An AI chatbot for customer messages is an assistant that reads what a customer types freely on Facebook, Instagram or your website, finds the answer in the information you gave it, replies in your voice, and hands over to a real person when a question is outside its scope. It can answer at midnight, when the whole team has their hands full, and it never forgets a question. But it is only as good as what you prepare for it. This guide explains how it works, what you need to prepare, and how to test it before real customers see it.

How an AI chatbot for customer messages differs from old auto-replies

Old auto-replies follow a script. The customer taps "Prices" and the machine sends the price list. The customer types the exact keyword and gets the prepared sentence. Type it slightly differently and the machine stays silent, or answers off topic. An AI chatbot reads and understands the whole sentence.

The two are not mutually exclusive. Many businesses use the greeting and away messages built into Meta's business tools or WhatsApp Business, then add an AI chatbot for freely typed questions. The scripted basics are covered in our guide to social media inbox automation.

How an AI chatbot works, step by step

  1. The customer sends a message. For example: "do u do dip, how much, any slot tonight 7pm".
  2. The AI reads it and separates the parts. Three parts: whether you do dip, the price, and whether 7pm tonight is free.
  3. The AI looks in the knowledge document. The first two are in the service list. The third needs the real calendar.
  4. The AI answers what it knows for certain. "Yes, we do dip, from ...". It does not guess the part it is unsure of.
  5. The AI hands the rest to a person. "I am passing your question about tonight to our front desk and they will reply right away."
  6. The conversation is recorded. At the end of the week you know what customers asked most and where the chatbot was stuck.

If the chatbot is connected to your booking calendar, it can answer the third part too. If it is not, do not let it accept bookings by itself. A wrongly accepted appointment is far worse than an answer that arrives ten minutes later.

This is the part that decides everything. The chatbot knows nothing about your business beyond what is in this document.

Where do the thirty questions come from? Open the last three months of your inbox and copy them. Do not make them up, because customers ask differently from what you expect. The chatbot's voice should use the same document as your posts; see our guide on teaching AI your brand voice.

What an AI chatbot must never do alone

  • Handle complaints. It records the complaint, apologises for the inconvenience and passes it straight to someone with the authority to decide.
  • Answer health or safety questions. Allergies, pregnancy, skin conditions. A real person answers and, when needed, advises the customer to ask a qualified professional.
  • Quote prices that are not on the list, give discounts or make promises. A chatbot can be talked into agreeing to a discount very easily. Forbid it outright.
  • Accept bookings when it is not connected to the real calendar.
  • Collect sensitive information. Card numbers, identity documents, health details.

Customers should know they are talking to an automated assistant. An opening line such as "I am the shop's automated assistant, and I will pass anything I am unsure about to the team" is honest and makes the handover feel natural. In some places the law also requires this disclosure.

Test before you switch on: 20 questions

Do not switch it on for customers before testing it yourself. Message the chatbot as a demanding customer would, using the seven groups below.

Out-of-scope questions3Say honestly that it has no information and hand over
Requests for a discount or an exception2Not agree, not promise, hand over
Complaints2Record, not argue, hand over at once
Questions in another language2Reply in the customer's language

A single answer that invents a fact means it is not ready. Fix the knowledge document or tighten the limits, then run the test again from the start.

Handing over to a real person smoothly

  • Tell the customer. "I am passing this to our manager, who will reply during opening hours." Customers will wait, as long as they know who they are waiting for and for how long.
  • Hand over with a summary. The person taking over sees what the customer asked and what was already answered, so the customer does not have to repeat it.
  • Make sure someone receives it. Decide who watches the inbox and when. A handover that nobody reads is worse than having no chatbot.
  • Out of hours. The chatbot states your working hours and records the request. It does not promise on a person's behalf.

Each platform has its own rules about how long a business may keep replying to a customer and about automated messages. These rules change, so check the Instagram Help Center and each platform's official pages before you set anything up.

What to review each week

  1. Questions the chatbot could not answer. Add the correct answer to the knowledge document.
  2. Answers that were not right. Wrong tone, too long, something missing. Fix the document, not each reply.
  3. The topics customers asked about most. Ten people asking about parking in one week means you should pin that information to your page and make a post about it.
  4. Conversations handed to a person. Were they answered in time, was any forgotten.

This review takes about fifteen minutes, and it is the reason the chatbot in month three is much better than in month one.

Common mistakes

  • Switching on a chatbot with no knowledge document. It will answer vaguely or guess.
  • Letting the chatbot handle complaints. An upset customer needs a real person.
  • Hiding that it is a machine. When customers find out, trust is lost.
  • Changing prices and forgetting the document. The chatbot quotes the old price with full confidence.
  • Nobody reading the handovers.

Frequently asked questions

Can an AI chatbot give wrong answers?

It can, especially when the knowledge document is incomplete or out of date. Reduce the risk by limiting it to answers from your own document, forbidding guesses when it is unsure, and reviewing conversations every week.

Should customers be told they are talking to AI?

Yes. Say in the first line that it is the shop's automated assistant. Customers accept it more easily, the handover to a person feels natural, and in some places the law requires it.

Which platforms can an AI chatbot work on?

Most commonly the messages of a Facebook Page, Instagram and a chat window on a website. Each platform has its own rules and way of connecting, so check its official pages before you set it up.

Does a small shop with few messages need an AI chatbot?

If you receive only a few messages a day and reply in time, the built-in greeting and away messages are enough. An AI chatbot is worth it when many messages arrive out of hours, or when a busy team keeps customers waiting.

How long does it take to set up an AI chatbot?

The longest part is writing the knowledge document, which usually takes a few sessions if you copy real questions from your inbox. After that comes the twenty-question test and adjusting until no invented answers remain.
