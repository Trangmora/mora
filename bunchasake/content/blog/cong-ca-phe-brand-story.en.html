In 2007 a small cafe opened on Trieu Viet Vuong Street in Hanoi. Its founder, Linh Dung, did not try to build a modern coffee shop. She rebuilt her childhood: army green walls, worn wooden furniture, objects from Vietnam's subsidy era. Almost twenty years later that cafe is Cong Ca Phe, one of the few Vietnamese coffee chains with stores in several countries.

Its story is worth reading for anyone running a restaurant or cafe chain, or a brand that wants to go abroad, because Cong does one very hard thing well: it keeps a single story intact across dozens, then hundreds, of stores.

Selling a memory, not a cup of coffee

Good coffee is not rare in Vietnam. A reason to choose one cafe over the next is. Cong's answer is memory: you walk in and step into another time, whether you lived it or only heard about it from your parents. For visitors from abroad it is a Vietnam they will not find in any other coffee shop.

That is positioning in its plainest form. Cong does not compete on price or beans, but on a feeling only it offers. Once the positioning is that clear, every later decision gets easier, from the chairs to the menu.

Identity lives in the experience, not only in the logo

Ask customers about Cong and few describe the logo. They describe the green, the wooden chairs, the staff uniforms, the coconut coffee. According to press coverage, the founder handled almost every detail herself, from menus and interiors to uniforms and merchandise. The result is an identity customers feel with all five senses, and one that can be copied in any store.

Merchandise is part of the story too. Customers take the memory home, and each object keeps telling the brand somewhere else.

Going abroad: choosing markets by where the customers come from

On 31 July 2018 Cong opened its first store outside Vietnam, in Seoul's Yeonnam-dong, a street popular with young Koreans (VnExpress International). Press reports at the time put the chain at more than 50 stores in Vietnam's major cities. Korea was chosen because Korean travel to Vietnam was growing fast and its coffee culture is strong: many Koreans already knew Cong from trips to Hanoi and Da Nang.

After Seoul, Cong opened in Kuala Lumpur in 2019. In November 2023 it opened its first North American store, in Toronto (Vietcetera). In early 2025 it announced its entry into the Philippines (Inside Retail Asia), and by 2026 it had added a store in Jakarta, Indonesia.

The lesson: Cong did not change its story for each country. A store in Seoul or Toronto is still a Hanoi memory. That consistency makes foreign customers curious and lets the Vietnamese diaspora recognise a corner of home.

Lessons for growing chains

  • One idea, kept for a long time. Cong chose subsidy-era memory and did not drop it when trends changed. Your chain needs one short positioning line every branch understands.
  • Write the guidelines before you copy the store. Colours, materials, uniforms, voice and service need to be written down so branch 50 still looks like branch one. This is also when identity drift happens most.
  • Franchising needs a story that is easy to copy. Partners in another country can only protect a brand that has been written down clearly.
  • Community follows the story. Regulars, travellers and the diaspora tell your story for free. See how to build a community around a brand.

Frequently asked questions

When and where was Cong Ca Phe founded?

In 2007, on Trieu Viet Vuong Street in Hanoi. The founder is Linh Dung.

Where was Cong's first store abroad?

In Yeonnam-dong, Seoul, South Korea, opened on 31 July 2018. The chain later opened in Malaysia, Canada, the Philippines and Indonesia.

Can a small chain learn from Cong?

Yes. The biggest lesson costs little: choose one clear story, write it into guidelines and keep it at every touchpoint before opening more branches.
