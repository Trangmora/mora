To teach AI your brand voice you need three things: a one-page voice document, a few real sample posts of your own, and a careful round of corrections on the first ten drafts. No special technique is required. The hard part is sitting down and writing out what you already do by habit: how you address customers, which words you use, which sentences you would never say. This guide gives you a seven-part template to copy and fill in, with a filled-in example for a nail salon.

Why AI writes posts that could belong to anyone

An AI assistant has read a great many adverts. When you only say "write a caption for a nail salon", it writes the average of all of them: a rhetorical question or two, some big adjectives, a vague call to action. The post is not wrong, but it is not yours.

The AI is not lazy. It simply lacks information. Wherever you say nothing, it fills in. Your job is to say enough that it has nothing left to guess.

The one-page brand voice document has 7 parts

  1. Who you are. Two sentences: what you sell, to whom, and where. No mission or vision statement needed.
  2. How you address customers. What you call yourselves and what you call them: "we" or the shop's name, first names or something more formal.
  3. Three traits, each explained. Every trait comes with a "this means" and a "this does not mean". If you only write "friendly", the AI reads it in its own way.
  4. Words you use and words you ban. Five to ten words you actually say to customers, and the words you never want to see under your name.
  5. Post format. Length, whether to use emoji, how many hashtags, whether capitals are used for emphasis, how a post ends.
  6. Facts it may use and what it must never say. The price list, opening hours, policies. Plus one rule: when information is missing, mark it, never guess.
  7. Three to five sample posts. Real posts you published and like best. This is the part the AI learns most from.

All seven parts should fit on one page. Any longer and you will not reread it yourself, and the important points get buried.

A filled-in example for a nail salon

Here is a filled-in example. Replace the details with your own and keep the short, specific style.

PartFilled-in example
Who you areA nail salon with four technicians in [neighbourhood], specialising in gel and dip. Most customers work nearby and come in after office hours.
How you address customersSay "we" or "the salon". Call customers "you". Use first names in replies. Never "valued customer".
Three traitsFriendly: talk as you would to a regular, not like a flyer. Clear: give the price and the time, no detours. Kind: never criticise other salons, never pressure a customer.
Words you use"pop in", "message us", "book ahead", "this set", "this shade on you"
Words you ban"super hot", "blowout sale", "cheapest", "guaranteed", "today only", any promise about how long nails last
FormatUnder 60 words. One emoji at most. No words in all capitals. Three hashtags. Closing line: "Message us to book."
FactsUse only prices from the attached price list. Opening hours: 9am to 7pm. If information is missing, write [CHECK], never guess.
Sample postsPaste three to five posts you published and like best.

Look at the traits. "Friendly" comes with its opposite, so the AI knows what to avoid. And the facts section has a rule for marking gaps, so you can see at a glance what needs checking.

How to choose sample posts

  • Real posts that were published. Do not ask AI to write the samples and then teach it from them.
  • Posts you like, not necessarily the ones with the most likes. Promotion posts often get more interaction, but if you use them as samples, every post becomes a promotion.
  • Different kinds. One introducing a service, one behind the scenes, one announcement, one answering a customer's question.
  • Nothing you like yet? Write three posts in the words you use with customers. Read them aloud. If they sound like you, use them.

The first ten drafts: the correction round

Give the document to an AI assistant, for example Claude, and ask for ten trial posts. Edit each one and say exactly what is wrong. "Make it better" does not help. "Remove the opening question, put the main fact in the first sentence" does.

Common problem in a draftRule to add to the document
Opens with a rhetorical question"The first sentence states the main fact. Never open with a question."
Too many adjectives"Two adjectives at most per post. Describe with real details, not praise."
Too formal"Write as if texting a regular customer."
Adds its own information"Use only facts in this document. If something is missing, write [CHECK]."
Vague ending"Always end with one specific thing the customer can do now."

A simple rule: when you have to correct the same mistake a third time, write it down as a rule and add it to the document. One voice, several channels, two languages

The voice is one. The format changes by channel. Add a small table like this to the document.

ChannelAdjustment
FacebookA little longer, with price, hours and how to book
InstagramShort, and the first line must stand alone
TikTokOne line that says what the video does not
Replies to customer messagesVery short: answer the question first, invite second

If you post in two languages, do not translate the document. Write the word lists and sample posts separately for each language, because a casual tone works very differently in Vietnamese and in English. Check the voice in five minutes

  1. Hide the name. Take three posts the AI wrote for you and two from other businesses, remove the names, and ask your staff which are yours. If they get them all right, the voice is clear.
  2. Read aloud. Any sentence you feel awkward saying will sound forced to customers too.
  3. Ask "would we say this at the counter?" If not, change it.

However good the voice, a post still has to be useful to the reader. That is also what Google stresses in its guidance on helpful, reliable content.

When to update the document

  • When you correct the same mistake a third time.
  • When a price, opening hours or a service changes. Update it the same day.
  • When you publish a post you really like: swap it in for the oldest sample.
  • Every three months, reread the whole page once and delete lines that are no longer true.

Common mistakes

  • Writing only adjectives. "Young, professional, approachable" says nothing without examples.
  • A ten-page document. Nobody rereads it, including you.
  • Sample posts written by AI. The AI learns its own average voice back.
  • Fixing the post but not the document. The same mistake returns next week.
  • Forgetting the facts section. The right voice with the wrong price is still a failed post.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a brand voice document be?

One page is enough for a small business: seven short parts and three to five sample posts. Longer documents are rarely reread and dilute what matters.

How many sample posts does AI need to pick up a voice?

Three to five real posts of different kinds are usually enough to start. The rest comes from correcting the first ten drafts and writing down the rules.

Which AI assistant should I use to write in my brand voice?

This method works with the widely used AI assistants. What decides the result is the document and the samples you give it, not the name of the tool.

Where do I get sample posts if I have never posted?

Write three posts in the words you use with customers at the counter, then read them aloud. Any that sound like you can be samples. Do not use AI-written posts as samples.

How do several people on a team keep one voice when they all use AI?

The whole team uses one shared document, and only one person may edit it. Anyone who sees a repeated mistake tells the document's keeper, who adds a rule.
