What is GEO? GEO, short for generative engine optimization, is optimising so that AI assistants name, quote or link to your brand when customers ask. Customers no longer only type "nail salon near me" into Google. They ask ChatGPT, ask Gemini, or read the AI answer at the very top of the results page. This guide explains GEO in plain words, reports what Google and researchers have published, and then gives seven things a small business can do itself, without being technical.

What is GEO, and how it differs from SEO

SEO, search engine optimization, is making sure customers who search Google find your page in the list of results. GEO is making sure an AI mentions you in the answer it writes. Some people call it AEO, answer engine optimization. Two names, one idea.

SEOGEO
What the customer doesTypes a few keywords, picks a linkAsks a full question, reads an answer the AI has put together
What you wantYour page high in the listYour name mentioned, your page linked as a source
RankingThere is a position 1, 2, 3No position 1, only being mentioned more or less often
WhereGoogle, BingGoogle's AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity
FoundationA useful, readable, indexed pageThe same, plus: direct answers, facts, sources, mentions elsewhere

The table shows that both share one foundation. That is also what Google says.

What Google says about GEO

In May 2026, Google published a dedicated guide to optimising your website for generative AI features on Google Search. Its main point: for Google Search, AEO and GEO are not separate disciplines. They are still SEO.

Google's documentation page AI features and your website is more specific. To be shown as a link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, your page only needs to be indexed by Google and eligible to appear with a snippet in Google Search. There are no additional requirements and no special optimisation is needed.

As Search Engine Journal summarises it, Google also considers an llms.txt file, breaking content into chunks, rewriting pages specially for AI and special structured data unnecessary for appearing in its AI features.

In June 2026, Google began testing a control in Search Console that lets website owners manage how their pages appear in AI features, together with separate reporting on appearances in AI answers. The first phase is limited to a group of website owners in the United Kingdom.

That is Google. ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity read the web and choose sources in their own ways, and none of them publishes a formula. So everything below is foundation work that helps everywhere, not a trick for one tool.

What research says about GEO

The term GEO comes from the research paper GEO: Generative Engine Optimization by a group at Princeton University and collaborators, presented at the KDD conference in 2024. The team tried many ways of editing content across a large set of questions and measured how much of that content AI used in its answers.

The finding: optimisation methods can raise visibility in AI answers by up to 40%, and the effect varies by field. The three methods that worked most clearly were adding cited sources, adding quotations from real people and adding statistics. Keyword stuffing did almost nothing.

This is a research experiment, not a promise about results on Google or ChatGPT. But the direction is clear: AI favours content with facts that can be checked.

7 things a small business can do itself

  1. Answer directly at the top of the page. Each page answers one question customers often ask, and the answer sits in the first paragraph. A nail salon's service page should open with where the salon is, what it does, prices from how much, opening hours and whether walk-ins are welcome.
  2. Write real, specific facts. Prices, opening hours, address, how long a service takes, materials. When you quote a number from elsewhere, name the source. If you have no number, do not invent one.
  3. Build a questions page in your customers' own words. Take the questions from real messages and comments. "Does dip damage nails?", "Is there parking?". One short, complete answer for each.
  4. Keep the SEO foundation. The page is indexed by Google, loads fast, reads well on a phone, has a title that says what it is about, and does not block search engines in its robots.txt file. Start with Google's SEO Starter Guide.
  5. Keep your details identical everywhere. Name, address, phone number and opening hours must match on your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram. When an AI finds two versions, it may pick the wrong one or leave you out.
  6. Get mentioned elsewhere. Genuine customer reviews, local press, blogs, neighbourhood groups. AI draws on many sources, not only your website. Do not buy reviews and do not ask anyone to write fake ones.
  7. Post consistently and keep things current. New posts, real photos, videos filmed in your shop. Correct old information that is now wrong, and show the date it was updated.

What not to do: stuff keywords, hide text in the page, or build pages only to "trick" AI. Google classes those as spam. Point 7 is the hardest to keep up, and it is where an automated system helps most, as our guide to what AI marketing automation is explains.

Check your GEO in 15 minutes

Open ChatGPT, Gemini and Google and ask the questions below in turn. Replace the parts in brackets with your own details.

Test questionWhat you are checking
"What is [brand name] and where is it?"Whether AI knows you, and whether the facts are right
"Which [type of business] is good in [area]?"Whether you are mentioned, and who else is
"How much does [service] cost in [area]?"Where AI takes its prices from
"Compare [you] with [another name in the area]"Which words AI uses to describe you

Write the answers down and ask again once a month. AI answers differ each time, so look at the trend over several tries and do not draw a conclusion from one. When a fact is wrong, fix it at the source, meaning your website and your Google Business Profile. Arguing with the AI achieves nothing.

How to measure GEO

  • Google Search Console. Impressions and clicks from Google's AI features are counted within the overall performance report. Separate reporting for AI is being tested by Google. Sign up free at Google Search Console.
  • Website analytics. See whether any visits arrive from AI assistant websites.
  • Ask new customers. Add "asked an AI" as an option to "How did you hear about us?".

Do not look for "position 1". AI answers have no fixed ranking. The practical measure is how many questions you are mentioned in, and whether what is said about you is correct.

Common mistakes

  • Treating GEO as a new trick that replaces SEO. No trick rescues a weak foundation.
  • Buying an expensive tool before fixing the basics. Correcting wrong opening hours on Google Maps helps far more.
  • Saying different things in different places. One phone number on the website, another on Facebook.
  • Writing a lot without answering the question. Both AI and readers skip it.
  • Inventing statistics or praise. A few weeks of speed, a long loss of trust.

Frequently asked questions

Does GEO replace SEO?

No. According to guidance Google published in May 2026, optimising for Google's AI features is still SEO. If you do SEO properly, you have already covered most of GEO.

Do I need an llms.txt file for AI to read my website?

Not for Google, according to its own guidance. Some other tools may read the file and having one does no harm, but it should not come before your content and your basic business details.

How long until AI mentions my brand?

Nobody can promise a timeline, because each AI assistant reads the web in its own way and publishes no formula. What you control is correct information, content that answers questions directly, and showing up consistently.

How do I do GEO without a website?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, social pages with correct details and genuine customer reviews. You should still have a website, because it is the only place where you fully control the information about you.

What do I do when AI says something wrong about my business?

Find where the wrong fact lives: an old website, the Google Maps listing, a directory. Fix it at the source, then ask again after some time. There is no way to edit an AI answer directly.
