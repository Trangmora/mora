Social media inbox automation for small business means letting a prepared message answer the simple, repeated questions in your Facebook, Instagram, TikTok or WhatsApp inbox, so that your team has time for the conversations that need a person. The rule is short. Automate the questions that have one true answer. Hand everything else to a human, and say clearly when that human will reply. This guide shows how to sort your messages, what to write, and where automation must stop.

What inbox automation really is

The word sounds technical, but for an owner-run business it usually means four simple things. You can use one of them or all four.

  • Saved replies. Answers you write once and send with a tap. A person still chooses and sends them. This is the safest place to start.
  • A greeting message. A short welcome sent automatically when someone writes to you for the first time.
  • An away message. An automatic note outside opening hours that says when you will answer.
  • Answers to frequent questions. A short menu of questions the customer can tap, or a reply triggered by a word such as "hours". Some businesses add an AI assistant here that reads the message and drafts or sends an answer.

Most platforms offer some of these to business accounts. The names, the options and who can use them differ between platforms and change over time, so check the official pages before you plan: Meta's business tools for Facebook and Instagram, WhatsApp Business and TikTok for Business.

What to automate: questions with one true answer

A question is safe to automate when three things are true. The answer is the same for every customer. The answer rarely changes. A wrong answer would be a small annoyance, not a problem.

For a salon with four technicians in a strip mall or a family phở restaurant with a lunch crowd, that list is usually:

  • Opening hours, including the day you are closed.
  • Address, parking and the nearest landmark.
  • How to book or reserve, and whether walk-ins are welcome.
  • Where to see the full menu or list of services.
  • Which languages your team speaks.
  • Payment methods you accept.
  • A thank you and "we will answer by" note when you are closed.

To find your own list, open your inbox and read the last fifty messages. Write down each question once and put a mark next to it every time it comes back. The five or six questions with the most marks are your first saved answers.

What never to automate

Some messages look simple and are not. An automatic answer to these can cost you a customer, or worse.

  • Complaints. "My nails lifted after three days." "We waited forty minutes." An unhappy customer who receives a cheerful automatic reply becomes angrier. A person answers, by name, and offers to continue by phone or private message. Our nail salon review response examples show the tone that works.
  • Prices, quotes and discounts. A price in writing is a promise. Custom nail art, a catering order for sixty people and "can you do it cheaper" all depend on details. A person checks and answers.
  • Health and safety. Allergies, ingredients, pregnancy, skin or nail conditions, hygiene, a reaction after a visit. Never let a prepared message or an AI guess. Say only what you know to be true, and invite the customer to call.
  • Changes and cancellations. Moving an appointment, a late arrival, a deposit or a refund. Someone has to look at the real calendar.
  • Anything emotional or unusual. A birthday surprise, a condolence order, a message from a journalist, a job application.

If you are unsure where a question belongs, treat it as a human one.

How to write automatic replies that sound like you

A good automatic reply is short, true and honest about being automatic. Four rules:

  1. Say it is automatic. Customers forgive a prepared message. They do not forgive a machine pretending to be a person.
  2. Give one fact and one next step. Not five paragraphs.
  3. Say when a person will answer. And keep that promise.
  4. Write the way you speak at the counter. If your customers write in Vietnamese and English, prepare both.

Templates to adapt. Replace the parts in brackets with your own facts.

Greeting: "Hi, thank you for writing to [business name]. This is an automatic message. Tell us what you need and someone from our team will answer during opening hours, [days and hours]."

Away message: "We are closed right now. This is an automatic reply. We open again at [time] and will answer your message then. To book, [how to book]."

Hours and address: "We are open [days and hours], closed on [day]. You will find us at [address], with parking [where]. Walk-ins are welcome when we have a free chair."

Vietnamese version: "Dạ tiệm cảm ơn anh chị đã nhắn. Đây là tin nhắn tự động. Tiệm mở cửa [ngày, giờ], nghỉ [ngày]. Anh chị cần đặt lịch thì [cách đặt lịch]. Nhân viên sẽ trả lời anh chị trong giờ mở cửa."

Holding reply for a human question, sent by a person: "Thank you for telling us. I am [name], I want to check this properly and will come back to you today before [time]."

The handoff: how a message reaches a person

Automation fails at the handoff more often than at the reply. Decide these four things and write them down.

  • Who owns the inbox. One named person per shift, not "everyone".
  • When it is checked. For example at opening, after the lunch rush and before closing. Three fixed moments are better than a phone that interrupts every service.
  • How fast a person replies. Choose a promise you can keep, such as "the same working day", and use those words in the away message.
  • What goes straight to the owner. Complaints, health questions and anything about money.

If an AI assistant drafts replies for you, treat it like a new employee in the first week. It prepares a draft, a person reads it and presses send. Give it a written list of the facts it may use and tell it to hand over when a message is outside that list. Our guide to an AI marketing assistant for small business explains how to brief one.

Set it up in one afternoon

  1. Read the last fifty messages and list the repeated questions.
  2. Sort them with the table above.
  3. Write a saved answer for each automatic one, in each language you serve.
  4. Write the greeting and the away message.
  5. Enter them in each platform you use and send yourself a test message from another account.
  6. Write the handoff rules on one page and show them to the team.
  7. Put a monthly reminder in the calendar to check every saved answer against reality.

Common mistakes

  • Old facts. Holiday hours change, the automatic reply does not. A customer who drives to a closed door will not message again.
  • A reply that sounds human but is not. Always say when a message is automatic.
  • No way out. Every automatic answer should end with how to reach a person.
  • Forgetting comments. Questions arrive under posts as well as in private messages. Someone should read both.

Frequently asked questions

Will customers be annoyed by automatic replies?

Not if the reply is short, useful and honest about being automatic. Customers are annoyed when a prepared message ignores what they asked or pretends to be a person.

Can an AI assistant answer my whole inbox?

It should not. An AI assistant can draft or send answers to simple factual questions from a list you approve, but complaints, prices and anything about health or safety need a person every time.

Should an automatic reply ever mention prices?

We advise against it. Prices change and depend on details, so let the automatic message thank the customer and let a person confirm any figure.

How often should I update my saved answers?

Check them once a month and every time something changes, such as holiday hours, a new booking method or a new address.
