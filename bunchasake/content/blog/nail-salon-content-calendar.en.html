A nail salon content calendar is a single page that says what you will post, on which day, on which platform and who will do it, for the next four weeks. It replaces the daily question "what do we post today?" with one hour of planning a month. Below is a monthly plan you can copy as it is, a seasonal planner for the year, and the routine that keeps it going when the salon is full.

What goes in a content calendar

You do not need software. A sheet of paper on the back-room wall works. So does a shared spreadsheet the whole team can open on their phones. What matters is the columns.

Three lists to write before you plan

A calendar copied without your own dates is just a list. Take ten minutes with the appointment book and write these down first.

  1. Fixed dates. Public holidays, school holidays, proms or formals, local events, the days the salon is closed, technicians' time off. Dates such as Mother's Day differ by country, so check your own.
  2. Quiet days and full days. Look at the last two months. If Mondays and Tuesdays are thin, those are the days that need an openings post or an offer, published two or three days earlier. If the week before a holiday always fills up, clients need a "book early" post two weeks ahead.
  3. One or two services to sell. Not everything. Pedicures before sandal weather, a new technician's speciality, a design set for the coming holiday.

Two things stay outside the table because they cannot be planned. A last-minute opening goes into Stories the moment someone cancels. A lovely set that walks out of the door on a Tuesday can replace any "best set" slot.

Short on time? Keep only Monday, Wednesday and Saturday. Twelve posts kept all year are better than sixteen kept for six weeks.

A simple rule for any of these: a first post early enough that clients can still choose their slot, a reminder about a week before, and holiday hours on every page. Put the hours on your Google Business Profile too, since that is where many people check whether you are open.

The one-hour monthly routine

Pick a quiet moment in the last week of the month. One person leads, and the team adds ideas.

  1. Look back, 10 minutes. Which posts brought messages, calls or bookings? What did clients ask at the desk? Circle two things to repeat.
  2. Write the dates, 10 minutes. The three lists above go into the calendar first.
  3. Fill the 16 slots, 20 minutes. Copy the table, then make each line specific: which colours, which technician, which offer.
  4. Write week one, 20 minutes. Captions for the first four posts, in the three-line shape: what it is, who did it, how to book.

Then give filming its own small slot each week. Half an hour on your quietest morning is enough to shoot three or four clips and photos for the days ahead.

In a salon with four technicians, roles can be simple. One person films. The owner reads every caption before it goes out. Whoever sits at the front desk replies to comments and messages. Write the names in the "Who" column so nothing is left to "someone".

Schedule ahead so busy days do not break the plan

Once the captions are written, most posts can be loaded in advance. Facebook and Instagram posts can be scheduled with the free tools at Meta for Business. TikTok offers scheduling on some accounts, and the options change, so check TikTok for Business for what yours can do. Keep Stories and last-minute openings live, because they depend on today.

When the plan breaks

It will. A technician is ill, a wedding party fills Saturday, the phone that holds the videos falls in the pedicure basin.

  • Keep three spare posts ready. The price list, the walk from the car park to your door, one aftercare tip. They are true every week of the year.
  • Skip, do not double up. A missed Wednesday does not become two posts on Thursday.
  • Move, do not delete. An idea that did not get filmed goes to next month's list.
  • Protect Monday. If only one post survives the week, make it the one about openings and hours. It is the post closest to a booking.

Common mistakes

  • Planning for a bigger team than you have. Thirty posts on paper, eight in real life. Start with twelve.
  • A calendar full of offers. Clients learn to wait for the discount. One offer a month, aimed at your quiet days, is enough.
  • The plan lives in one person's head. When the owner is away, posting stops. Put the calendar where the team can see it.
  • Remembering the holiday in the week of the holiday. By then the chairs are either full or the clients have booked elsewhere.
  • Vague lines. "Post nails" is not a plan. "Milky pink almond by Trang, photo by the window" is.
  • Never looking back. Without the ten-minute review, you repeat what was easy, not what worked.

Frequently asked questions

How many posts a month does a nail salon need?

Twelve to sixteen planned posts is a realistic range for a small salon, plus Stories on days with openings. Choose the number your team can keep for a whole year.

How far ahead should we plan?

Plan one month in detail and keep a simple list of the year's big dates. Planning three months in detail usually goes out of date before you use it.

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

Use whatever your team will really open. Paper on the wall is fine for one location. A shared spreadsheet helps when the owner is not in the salon every day.

Should the calendar be in English or Vietnamese?

Write the plan in the language your team reads most easily, often Vietnamese, and write the captions in the language of your clients, usually English.

What if a trend appears that is not in the plan?

Swap it into the nearest "best set" or video slot. The calendar is there so you never start from an empty page, not to stop a good idea.

Can AI write the whole calendar for us?

AI can draft ideas and captions quickly, but it does not know your quiet days, your technicians or your clients unless you tell it. Give it your three lists and read every draft before it is posted.
