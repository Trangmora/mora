In late 2009 Domino's did something few chains dare to do: it went on television to say customers disliked its pizza, and that they were right. The "Pizza Turnaround" campaign used real criticism from customer interviews, then showed the kitchen team rebuilding the recipe from scratch. The D&AD awards summed up the idea in two words: come clean (D&AD).

The Domino's story is worth reading for owners of restaurant chains, cafés and delivery brands, because it shows two things working together: listening to customers, and serving them where they already are.

Daring to hear the complaints

Many brands treat complaints as something to hide. Domino's did the opposite: it put the complaints in its ads, then proved it had fixed the problem. The result was measurable. In Q1 2010, U.S. same store sales rose 14.3%, and the company said this came from more customers after the new pizza launched (Domino's Q1 2010 results).

The lesson is not "criticise yourself". It is that customers are already telling the truth about you somewhere: on social media, in messages, in reviews. The brand that reads them early and answers honestly gets ahead.

Taking ordering to where customers message

After fixing the product, Domino's fixed how people order. It called the programme AnyWare. On 20 May 2015, customers with a saved favourite order could tweet a pizza emoji to @Dominos and the order was placed. A month later it added ordering by text message, alongside smart TVs, smartwatches, cars and a voice assistant (Domino's).

According to the company, about 50% of its U.S. sales came from digital channels by the end of 2014. A pizza emoji sounds like a gimmick, but the idea behind it is serious: the fewer steps it takes to order, the easier it is for customers to come back.

A brand lives in every conversation

To customers, Domino's is not just a pizza. It is an ad that told the truth, an emoji sent at 11pm, a message confirming the order. Every one of those touchpoints is the brand. A chain with a great story that leaves customers waiting three hours for a reply loses the story too.

Lessons for growing restaurant chains

  • Read everything customers say. Comments, reviews and messages are a free customer interview every day. See how to collect and answer messages from every channel.
  • Fix it, then tell it. A story only carries weight when the product and service have really changed.
  • Remove steps. If customers are on Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp, let them ask prices, book a table or order right there, even at midnight.
  • Show up consistently. One post a week is not enough to be remembered. See how to post one piece of content across platforms.

Frequently asked questions

What was Domino's "Pizza Turnaround" campaign?

A campaign launched in late 2009 in which Domino's publicly admitted customers' complaints about its pizza, showed the recipe being rebuilt and invited people to try it again.

How did customers order Domino's with an emoji?

From May 2015, customers with a saved Easy Order on their Domino's account could tweet the pizza emoji or #EasyOrder to @Dominos to place the order.
