In 2022 the ketchup brand Heinz ran a small experiment: it typed "ketchup" and a few variations into the AI image generator DALL-E 2. The images that came back showed bottles with a shape, label colour and feel very close to a Heinz bottle. Heinz and its agency Rethink turned that into the "A.I. Ketchup" campaign (The Drum).

The story is worth reading for owners of restaurant chains, cafés or any growing brand, because it shows something that matters more every year: customers no longer only search Google, they ask AI. And AI only mentions the brands it "recognises".

Before AI, people: the "Draw Ketchup" experiment

A year earlier, in 2021, Heinz ran a similar experiment with people. It asked people in several countries to draw "ketchup" without saying which brand was asking. Most drew a Heinz bottle. Heinz printed those drawings on its labels and used them in ads, work recognised by the D&AD awards (D&AD).

What Heinz proved was not "our ketchup tastes best", but "we are the picture of ketchup in your head". Only years of consistent advertising can do that.

Why did AI draw Heinz too?

AI image generators learn from billions of images online. A brand that keeps the same bottle, the same label colour and the same way of showing up for decades leaves a dense trail in that data. So when asked for "ketchup", the AI draws what it has seen most.

The same applies to how customers find brands in 2026. When someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini "which pho place is good around here", the AI answers with what it has read most often and most consistently about places online.

Turning customers into co-creators

Heinz did not stop at a few images. It invited fans to write their own prompts, then put the best images on social media, outdoor ads, print and even special edition bottles (Muse by Clios). According to the agency, the campaign was mentioned hundreds of millions of times across press and social media; these are the agency's own figures.

It is a clever use of AI: the AI does not speak for the brand, it gives customers a reason to play with it.

Lessons for growing chains

  • Keep one identity on every channel. Same colours, same voice, same photo style on your website, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok. That is how customers, and AI, remember you correctly.
  • Show up consistently. Heinz has decades. A small chain does not, so posting every day matters even more. See how to use an AI assistant for content that still sounds like you.
  • Let customers play. A small challenge, a vote, a post inviting photos. Content made by customers is often trusted more than ads.
  • Check what AI says about you. Ask ChatGPT and Gemini about your trade and area. If your name is missing, that is your next job. Like Domino's, be where customers are asking.

Frequently asked questions

What was Heinz's "A.I. Ketchup" campaign?

A 2022 campaign in which Heinz asked the image generator DALL-E 2 to draw "ketchup"; the images looked very much like a Heinz bottle, and the brand used them as advertising and invited fans to write their own prompts.

Why did the AI draw Heinz?

Because AI learns from huge numbers of images online, and Heinz has kept the same identity for decades. A brand that shows up consistently and often is the one AI remembers.

What can a small chain learn from Heinz?

Keep one identity on every channel, post consistently, and invite customers to take part in your content. AI helps a small chain make more content while keeping its own voice.
