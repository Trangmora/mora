The nail salon TikTok content ideas that work are simple: one thing per video, filmed on a phone, with the town named out loud. TikTok shows videos to people who do not follow you. That is its gift and its trap, because a video can be watched a thousand miles from your chair. So this guide has two halves: 12 videos to film this month, and the steps that make a nearby viewer book.

How TikTok is different from your other pages

On Facebook and Instagram, most people who see a post already know the salon. On TikTok, most do not. That changes four things.

  • Every video stands alone. The viewer has not seen your profile. The salon name and the town must be in the video itself.
  • The first two seconds decide. If nothing happens, she swipes. Start with the action, not with a logo.
  • Sound is on. The file, the click of a bottle, your voice. These are part of the video.
  • People search inside TikTok. Many type things like "nails in" plus their town. The words you say, write on screen and put in the caption help them find you.

Set up the profile so a viewer can book

A viewer who likes a video taps your profile and gives it a few seconds. Make those seconds count.

  • Account type. A business account gives you extra tools, and the details are explained at TikTok for Business. Features such as a link in the profile or the sounds you may use can differ by country and account type, so check what your own account shows.
  • Name. Salon name plus town or neighbourhood: "Lotus Nails, Garden Grove".
  • Bio. Three short lines: what you do, where you are, how to book. "Gel, acrylic, pedicures. Next to the pharmacy on Main Street. Book with the link or call."
  • Link. Your booking page. If your account does not offer a link yet, write the phone number in the bio and name your Instagram.
  • Three pinned videos. How to find us, what our services include, our best set. A stranger should get it in three taps.
  • Same name everywhere. Many people leave TikTok and search the salon name on Google. Use the same name and photo as on your Google Business Profile so they know it is the same place.

12 nail salon TikTok content ideas with hooks

Each idea has the hook to write on screen, what to film and a caption to copy. Change the names and the town to your own.

  1. The full set, sped up. Hook: "60 minutes in 20 seconds." Film from above, phone on a small stand, from prep to top coat, then speed it up. Caption: "Full acrylic set by Trang at Lotus Nails, Garden Grove. Book with the link in our profile."
  2. Sound on. Hook: "Sound on." No talking. The file, the brush tapping the jar, the lamp beeping. Close shots, ten seconds each. Caption: "The sounds of a Tuesday morning at the salon."
  3. Something simple. Hook: "She said: something simple." Show the plain base, then the small detail that makes it special. Caption: "Simple does not mean boring. Nude gel with one fine gold line."
  4. Reply to a comment. Hook: the comment itself, shown on screen. Use the reply-with-video option if your app has it. A technician answers in her own words while working. Caption: "How long should gel last? Here is our honest answer."
  5. What is included. Hook: "What a gel manicure includes at our salon." Six quick clips: shape, cuticle care, base, colour, top coat, oil. Caption: "Every step, every time. Prices are in our profile."
  6. How to find us. Hook: "How to find Lotus Nails." Film the walk from the car park to your door, then to the chair. Caption: "Between the pharmacy and the bakery, free parking in front."
  7. You choose the colour. Hook: "Pick one: 1, 2 or 3." Three bottles, three swatches. Next week, film the winning colour on a client and reply to the comments that chose it. Caption: "Comment your number. We will do the winner on Friday."
  8. The rescue. Hook: "Broken on Thursday. Wedding on Saturday." The snapped nail, the repair, the hand afterwards. Caption: "We repair single nails. Message us and we will find you a slot."
  9. Open to close. Hook: "A Saturday at a nail salon." Eight clips of two seconds: lights on, towels, first client, lunch at the back, the busy hour, sweeping, lights off. Caption: "Thank you to everyone who sat in our chairs today."
  10. The technician's story. Hook: "I did my first set at 16, on my sister." Her voice over clips of her hands at work. An accent is fine. So is Vietnamese, with English text on screen. Caption: "This is Linh. Ask for her if you love French tips."
  11. Before your first visit. Hook: "3 things to know before your first appointment with us." Come with bare nails or tell us what is on them, bring a photo, tell us if you are in a hurry. Caption: "New here? Save this."
  12. Please do not do this at home. Hook: "Please stop peeling your gel." Show a gentle soak-off and explain in one sentence why peeling damages the nail. Caption: "Removal takes a few minutes with us. Your nails will thank you."

Turning views into bookings

A video with many views from another country fills no chair. These five habits keep TikTok tied to the appointment book.

  1. Say the place three times. In the caption, as text on screen, and with the location tag if your app offers one. "Nails in Garden Grove" is more useful than a string of general hashtags.
  2. End with one action. "Book with the link in our profile" or "Call the salon". Choose one and use it on every video so regular viewers learn it.
  3. Answer "where" and "how much" the same day. Reply in public with the town and a starting price, then invite her to message. Other viewers have the same question and will read your answer.
  4. Prepare one saved reply. Price list, hours, address, booking link, in one message your front desk can paste. If clients write to you on WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business lets you save quick replies.
  5. Ask every new client how she found you. Keep a sheet of paper by the till with four columns: Google, walked past, a friend, TikTok. A month of tally marks tells you more than a view count.

Do not chase the video that travelled far. Make more of the kind that brought messages from people nearby.

Sound, music and trends

The safest sound is your own: the salon at work, or a technician's voice. Business accounts are usually limited to music cleared for commercial use, and the choice differs by country, so use what the app offers your account and do not add songs you have no right to use.

A trend is worth joining only when you can link it to nails within a day or two and it suits the salon. If you have to explain the joke to your own team, skip it.

Common mistakes

  • No town anywhere. The video is lovely and nobody knows it is ten minutes from their house.
  • Posting other people's videos. It is someone else's work, and the client will expect that exact set from you.
  • Starting with a logo or a greeting. Two seconds gone. Start with the hands.
  • Stopping after two weeks. The first videos are practice. Give it three months of two or three videos a week before you judge.
  • Deleting quiet videos. A video with few views can still be found by search months later. Leave it.
  • A different salon on every platform. If the name, colours and tone change from TikTok to the shop sign, people doubt it is the same place. Settling those once is brand work, and it is what we do at Bunchasake: see brand and marketing for nail salons.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a nail salon post on TikTok?

Two or three videos a week is a pace most salons can keep. Regular posting for months matters more than a burst of daily videos followed by silence.

Do we have to show our faces or dance?

No. Hands, tools, colours and the salon itself are enough. A voice over the video adds warmth without anyone standing in front of the camera.

Should we speak English or Vietnamese in our videos?

Use the language most of your clients speak, which is usually English, and put the words on screen. A Vietnamese voice with English text also works well and shows who you are.

Can we post the same video on Instagram Reels?

Yes. Save the clip before you add TikTok's own text and effects, then upload that clean file to each platform and write the caption again for each one.

Our video got many views but no bookings. What went wrong?

Usually the viewers were not local, or the video did not say where you are and how to book. Add the town and one booking instruction to the next videos and check your tally sheet after a month.

Do we need to pay for ads on TikTok?

Not to begin with. First learn which of your videos bring local messages. Paid promotion is a separate decision for later, and it is not a service Bunchasake sells.
