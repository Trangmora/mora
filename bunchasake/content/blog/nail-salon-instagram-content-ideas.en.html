The nail salon Instagram content ideas worth your time do three jobs: they show your work, they tell a stranger how to book, and they keep regulars thinking of you between visits. Start with a profile that answers price, location and booking, then rotate Reels, carousels and Stories. This guide walks through each part, with 10 Reels you can film this week and a two-week plan.

It covers Instagram only. Facebook, TikTok, content pillars and how to ask a client before you film her hands are in our wider guide to social media content ideas for nail salons.

Set up the profile first: bio, link and Highlights

Someone who sees one of your Reels will tap through to your profile and decide in a few seconds. Let her find these things without scrolling:

  • Name field. Your salon name plus what you are and where, for example "Sen Nails | Nail salon in" followed by your neighbourhood. Instagram uses this line when people search.
  • Bio. Three short lines: services, hours, languages. "Gel, acrylic, pedicures. Open 7 days, walk-ins welcome. English and Vietnamese spoken."
  • Link. Put your booking page first. If you take bookings by phone, turn on the call button.
  • Professional account. A business or creator account is free and adds contact buttons and basic numbers on what people watch. Instagram for Business is the official place to start. The buttons on offer change from time to time, so check your own account or the Instagram Help Center.
  • Highlights. Stories vanish after 24 hours, Highlights stay. Make at least three: Prices (the menu, one service group per story), Booking (how to book, deposit or cancellation rules, walk-in times) and Location (the shopfront, parking, the walk from the car to your door). Then add Work by style and Reviews if you like.
  • Pinned posts. Pin your price list, how to book and your best set to the top of the grid.

Nail salon Instagram content ideas for Reels: 10 hooks and what to film

The hook is the first two seconds: a line of text on screen that gives someone a reason to stay. Many people watch without sound, so write it, do not only say it. Film vertically, near a window, and keep most Reels between 10 and 30 seconds.

  1. The reveal. Hook: "Wait for the top coat." Film: the last coat, the lamp, then the hand turning slowly in the light.
  2. Before and after in one take. Hook: "She came in like this." Film: the grown-out set, a towel across the lens, then the fresh set.
  3. Almond or coffin. Hook: "Same nail, two shapes." Film: two sample tips filed each way, held side by side.
  4. After you leave. Hook: "What happens to our tools after your appointment." Film: your real cleaning steps, ending with a sealed pouch opened at the table.
  5. The emergency. Hook: "Broke a nail two days before the wedding." Film: the break, the repair, then all ten nails matching.
  6. Do not peel. Hook: "Please stop peeling your gel." Film: a gentle soak-off, then the healthy nail underneath.
  7. Meet the technician. Hook: "This is Linh. Ask her for French." Film: Linh at her station, then three of her sets.
  8. Inside the spa pedicure. Hook: "Everything in our spa pedicure, step by step." Film: soak, scrub, massage, polish, five seconds each.
  9. Design of the month. Hook: "The set everyone asked for in December." Film: a time-lapse from bare nail to finished art.
  10. One question. Hook: "How long should gel last?" Film: hands at work, with your answer in three lines of text.

Music is optional. The music offered to a business account can differ from a personal one, so stay with what Instagram gives you. Choose a cover for every Reel, because the cover is what sits on your grid.

Carousel ideas clients save

A carousel is a post with several images that people swipe through. It suits anything a client wants to keep or show her technician. Make the first image a clear title and the last one how to book.

  • Five sets for the season. One design per image, with its name and starting price.
  • The price list. One service group per image: manicures, pedicures, extensions, nail art.
  • Your first visit. Arriving, choosing a colour, the service, paying, booking the next one.
  • Aftercare in five images. Oil, gloves, no peeling, when to come back, what to do if one lifts.
  • What clients say. One review per image, first name only, with your thanks.

Stories routines: daily openings, last-minute slots, polls

Stories are for today. Each one takes a minute and does not need to be polished.

  • The morning opening. Every open day, one photo of the salon with text: "Open until 7. Two spots left after 3pm."
  • The last-minute slot. The moment someone cancels: "1:30 just opened with Mai. Reply here to take it." Delete or update the story once the slot is taken.
  • Polls and question boxes. "Red or burgundy for December?" "Ask us anything about gel." The answers give you the topic for next week's Reel.
  • The booking link. Once or twice a week, a story with a link to your booking page.

Save the useful ones into Highlights so they keep working after 24 hours.

Grid consistency: why a clear brand look matters

A new visitor reads your first nine to twelve posts as one picture. If every post has a different background, filter and font, the page looks like several salons. If they share one look, she recognises you in her feed before she reads the name, and your Instagram matches the sign over your door and the menu on your desk.

  • One photo spot by the window and one background, every time.
  • No filter that changes the polish colour. Clients expect the real shade.
  • Two brand colours and one typeface for every text post and Reel cover.
  • A cover on every Reel. Instagram has changed how the grid crops images before, so look at your own profile after you post.

Hashtags and location tags in plain terms

A hashtag is a label that tells Instagram what a post is about. A few specific ones are enough:

  • Two or three for the service: #gelnails, #almondnails, #spapedicure.
  • Two or three for the place: your city or neighbourhood joined with "nails" or "nailsalon".
  • One of your own, the salon name, so clients can use it too.

Instagram's advice on how many hashtags to use has changed more than once, so do not chase a number.

For a salon that serves its own area, the location tag matters more. Add your salon's location to every post and Reel, and the location sticker to Stories, so people browsing that place can find you. Write the place in the caption as well: "gel manicure in" followed by your neighbourhood. People who search on Google will meet you through your Google Business Profile, which is a separate job: see our guide to setting up Google Business Profile for a nail salon.

Common Instagram mistakes

  • A bio with no location and no way to book. She likes the set and cannot tell where you are.
  • "DM for price." Many people will not ask. Show at least a starting price.
  • Reels that open with a logo. Open with the result or a question.
  • Bought followers. A follower in another country never books in your neighbourhood.
  • Slow replies. A direct message is a client standing at the front desk. Answer the same day.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need Reels, or are photos enough?

Photos are enough to show your work to people who have already found you. Reels are more likely to be shown to people who do not follow you yet, so one Reel a week is a sensible start.

Should the salon use a personal or a professional account?

Use a professional account, set to public and kept separate from your personal page. Clients need to see your posts without sending a follow request, and you get contact buttons.

How long before Instagram brings in bookings?

There is no honest fixed answer. What you control is a profile that answers price, location and booking, a steady rhythm, and asking every new client how she found you. Other ways to fill the book are in how to get more nail clients.

Can we reuse our Reels on TikTok and Facebook?

Yes. Upload the original video file from your phone to each app, not a downloaded copy with a logo on it. What fits each platform is covered in social media content ideas for nail salons.
