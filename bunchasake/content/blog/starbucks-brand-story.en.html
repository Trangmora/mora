When people talk about AI in marketing they usually picture machine-made images or machine-written adverts. The Starbucks story goes another way. The company uses AI where customers cannot see it, so that where customers can see, there are still people.

Deep Brew: the AI platform Starbucks has built since 2019

In 2019 Starbucks launched Deep Brew, its own AI platform. According to diginomica's progress report, Deep Brew does three jobs: it drives the brand's personalisation engine, optimises staffing in stores and manages inventory.

Deep Brew even reaches the coffee machines. In January 2020, FastCasual reported that the new generation of Mastrena espresso machines sends operating data to the support centre, and that Starbucks planned to install about 4,000 of them that year. A machine about to fail is known in advance, instead of when the counter has to stop serving.

None of this is glamorous. It is scheduling, ordering, maintenance and suggestions. But those are exactly the tasks that eat a store manager's time.

Where the data comes from: 34 million members

AI can only suggest well when it knows what a customer likes. For Starbucks, that source is the app and the Starbucks Rewards programme. According to Starbucks's investor dashboard, in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 the company had 34.2 million 90-day active members in the United States.

Each time a member orders, Starbucks learns a little more: what they drink, at what time, at which store. From that, the app suggests something suited to each person, instead of sending everyone the same promotion.

The lesson is not the figure of 34 million. The lesson is that Starbucks had one single place recording who its customers are before it thought about AI.

Green Dot Assist: AI behind the counter, not in front of the customer

In June 2025 Starbucks introduced Green Dot Assist, an AI assistant for baristas built on Microsoft's Azure OpenAI service. Staff ask it how to make a drink or what to do when a machine misbehaves, instead of leafing through a manual or calling a manager. The Register described the approach as AI to support baristas instead of replacing them, and Fortune called it a test case for the industry.

Starbucks could have put an AI screen out front to take orders. It chose the opposite: let AI carry the looking-up, so staff have free hands and a free mind for the customer.

Lessons for a growing chain

  • Put customer data in one place first. You do not need your own app. A list of regulars with their last visit and usual order is a start. See our guide to AI customer care for past customers.
  • Automate repeated work first. Starbucks began with scheduling and inventory. For a small chain, the repeated work is the daily post and the familiar questions in the inbox. See our guide to what AI marketing automation is.
  • Keep people where customers can see them. AI drafts, a person approves. AI answers a question about opening hours, a person answers a complaint.
  • One set of facts for every branch. The Starbucks assistant answers from the same source for every store. Your chain also needs one shared table of prices, hours and recipes, as our guide to marketing for multi-location chains describes.

You do not need a Starbucks budget to follow that order. The order is the lesson: data first, repeated work first, people in front.

Frequently asked questions

What is Starbucks Deep Brew?

It is the AI platform Starbucks launched in 2019, used to personalise suggestions for each customer, optimise staffing and manage inventory in stores.

Is Starbucks using AI to replace baristas?

Not according to what the company announced in 2025. The Green Dot Assist helper lets baristas look up recipes and solve problems faster, so they have more time for customers.

What can a small chain learn from how Starbucks uses AI?

Keep customer information in one place, automate repeated work such as posting and answering familiar questions, and keep people in the places where customers deal with you directly.
