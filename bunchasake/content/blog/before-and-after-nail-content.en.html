Before and after nail content turns viewers into bookings when three things are true: the two pictures match, the before is honest, and the caption tells the viewer what to ask for and how to book. A finished set shows your skill. A pair shows what one visit changes, and the viewer looks at the before and thinks "that is my hand right now". This guide covers what to shoot, how to shoot it, the words to use and the lines not to cross.

Seven before and afters worth making

You do not need a disaster. The best pairs are the everyday ones.

  1. Grow-out to fresh. Five or six weeks of gap at the cuticle, then the fill or new set. This is the client you see most, so it is the pair most viewers recognise.
  2. The broken nail. One snapped nail among nine good ones, then the repair. It tells people you take single-nail fixes.
  3. Removal done properly. Old gel or acrylic, then the clean natural nail after a gentle soak-off. It shows care, not only decoration.
  4. The natural manicure. No colour at all. Dry cuticles and uneven edges, then shaped, tidy nails with oil. Good for clients who think a salon is "not for them".
  5. A change of shape. Square to almond, long to short. Viewers who are unsure what suits them save these.
  6. The pedicure. Heels and toes before and after, on a clean towel. Few salons post it, and it sells one of your longest services.
  7. The slow one. A client growing out bitten nails over several visits. Post visit one, visit three and visit five, and say plainly how many weeks it took.

One more kind needs care: fixing work done somewhere else. Show the result if the client agrees, but never name or hint at the other salon. "She came in with lifting at the edges" is enough.

How to shoot a matching pair

If the before is dark and the after is bright, people sense a trick, even when your work is real. The whole method is to change nothing but the nails.

WhatBefore photoAfter photo
WhenAfter hand washing, before any filing or removalAfter top coat and oil, once the oil is wiped in
SpotYour photo spot near the windowThe same spot, marked with a piece of tape
BackgroundA plain towel in one colourThe same towel
PoseFingers relaxed and flat, or a soft fistThe same pose. Look at the before on your screen and copy it
Distance and anglePhone straight above, hand filling the frameThe same height and angle
LightDaylight, no flashDaylight, no flash. If it is now dark, use the same lamp for both next time
EditingNoneNone. Crop only

Make "photo first, then file" a habit at every station. The before is the picture salons forget, and it cannot be taken later. Take two or three, because one will be blurred.

Three formats: side by side, swipe and reveal

  • Side by side. One image, before on the left, after on the right, each with a small label. It works on Facebook, and it is worth adding to the photos on your Google Business Profile, where people compare salons before they call.
  • Swipe. A two-image carousel on Instagram. Put the after first, because the first image is what shows on your grid, and write "swipe for the before". How carousels and covers work is explained in the Instagram Help Center.
  • Reveal. A short video for TikTok and Reels. Two seconds of the before with the words "6 weeks of grow-out" on screen, a simple change such as a towel passing over the hand, then the after held still for three seconds. In video, the before goes first: curiosity keeps people watching. More video hooks are in our TikTok ideas for nail salons.

Use the same labels, the same lettering and the same corner for your logo every time. After ten posts, people know a pair is yours before they read the name.

Captions that lead to a booking

The picture gets attention. The caption does the selling. Four short parts are enough:

  1. What it was.
  2. What you did, and how long it took.
  3. Who did it.
  4. One way to book.

Three examples to copy:

Six weeks of grow-out. Gel fill and a new colour, 50 minutes, by Trang. Book with the link in our profile.

One broken nail, two days before a wedding. Single-nail repair, 15 minutes, by Linh. Message us and we will find you a slot.

Bitten nails, visit one and visit five. Twelve weeks, short overlays and a lot of patience from both of us. Ask for Mai when you book.

Naming the service matters. A viewer who likes the result needs to know what to ask for at the desk. The time matters too, because "do I have time for this?" is a question nobody types in the comments.

If you post prices, write "from" and the starting price for that service, and keep it in line with your price list. A Vietnamese line can sit under the English when it helps your Vietnamese clients, for example "Tiệm nhận sửa một móng, nhắn tin để hẹn giờ."

Be kind to the before and honest about the after

The before belongs to a real person who trusted you with her hands. Treat it that way.

  • Ask at the start. "Would you mind if I take a quick photo before we begin, so we can show the difference? Hands only, and I will show you both pictures before anything is posted." For a Vietnamese client: "Em chụp một tấm trước khi làm để so với lúc xong được không chị? Chỉ chụp tay thôi, đăng gì em cũng đưa chị xem trước."
  • Show her the pair. If she is not comfortable, do not post it. If she changes her mind later, take it down the same day.
  • No shaming words. Not "disaster", not "horror", no laughing emoji. Describe the nail, not the person: "grown out", "lifting", "broken at the side".
  • No medical cases. If a nail looks infected or the skin looks unwell, do not work on it, do not photograph it, and suggest she sees a doctor. Never write that a service treats, cures or heals anything.
  • No editing. No filters, no smoothing, no borrowed photos. If the result took five visits, say five visits.

Advertising has to be truthful, and before and after pictures count as advertising. The rules differ by country. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's business guidance is the official place to read about them. For a specific legal question, ask a qualified adviser where you operate.

Make it a habit, not a project

One pair a day from each technician gives a four-chair salon more material than it can post. A simple routine:

  • Every technician takes the before as part of setting up the table.
  • Photos go into one shared album on the salon phone, with the technician's name and the client's yes noted.
  • Once a week, the owner chooses the two clearest pairs and writes the captions.
  • One becomes a photo post, the other a short video.

Common mistakes

  • A dark before and a bright after. The difference in light does the work, and viewers notice.
  • A before taken halfway through. Once the old colour is off, the real starting point is gone.
  • Only extreme cases. They attract attention but also people who are not your clients. Ordinary grow-out fills more chairs.
  • No caption, or only hashtags. The viewer admires it and moves on with no idea how to book.
  • A big watermark over the nails. Protect your work with a small logo in a corner, not across the picture.
  • A new layout every time. Different labels, colours and lettering each week look like different salons. A fixed before and after template in your own colours is part of the post templates we design at Bunchasake, as part of brand and marketing for nail salons.

Frequently asked questions

Should the before or the after come first?

In a still image or carousel, lead with the after because it is the picture people see on your page. In a video, start with the before so the viewer stays to see the change.

Do we need the client's permission for a hands-only photo?

Yes. Ask before the before photo, show her the pair, and respect a no. Privacy rules differ by country, so a clear yes protects the client and the salon.

How often should we post a before and after?

Once or twice a week is plenty alongside your other posts. If every post is a transformation, people stop seeing your finished work, your team and your practical information.

Can we use a filter to make the colours look better?

No. Use daylight and a clean lens. A filtered after sets an expectation the next client will not get in the chair.

What if the client is embarrassed by her before?

Do not post it. You can post the after alone, or ask the next client. A regular who feels respected is worth more than one post.

Can we post a before and after of a nail fungus or infection?

No. That is a matter for a doctor, not a salon post. Decline the service kindly and suggest she gets it looked at first.
