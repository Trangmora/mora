20 hair salon social media content ideas that lead to bookings

A new client books when she trusts the stylist and can picture the result on hair like hers. These 20 posts, in four groups, show both and make booking easy.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • A hair salon needs four kinds of posts on rotation: transformations, education, the stylists, and clear booking information.
  • Show results on many hair types and lengths, because a new client books when she sees hair like her own.
  • Film every transformation in the same spot and the same light, show the back of the head, and never filter the colour.
  • Every post should name the service, the stylist and one way to book.
  • Ask for permission when you take the before photo, and offer to film only the back if the client prefers.

The hair salon social media content ideas that bring bookings answer two questions a new client is quietly asking: can this stylist do my hair, and how do I book? Four kinds of posts answer them: transformations, education, the stylists, and booking information. Below are 20 ideas in those groups, how to film a transformation honestly, and a content bank you can copy.

What a new client wants to see before she books a stylist

A haircut or a colour change feels risky. A bad result lasts for weeks and everyone sees it. So before booking, a new client scrolls your page looking for three things.

  • Hair like hers. If she has thick curly hair and your feed shows only straight blonde lengths, she assumes you are not for her.
  • A person she trusts. She is choosing a stylist more than a salon. Names, faces and specialties matter.
  • No surprises. What the service is called, roughly what it costs, how long it takes, and how to book.

Each group below answers one of these worries. Rotate them and your page does the work of a consultation before she walks in.

Hair salon social media content ideas: 20 posts in four groups

Each idea comes with what to film and a caption starter. Swap in your own names, services and hours.

Group 1: transformations

  1. The colour change. Before at the chair, after by the window, same angle. "Brassy to soft beige blonde. Five hours with Maya, worth every minute."
  2. The big cut. Long to short, filmed as a reveal when the chair turns. "Twelve inches off. She has wanted this bob for two years."
  3. Grey blending. Gentle and respectful, with her permission. "Not covering the grey, blending it. Softer grow-out, fewer visits."
  4. The curl cut. Curls before and after, shot from the side so the shape shows. "Cut curl by curl, so it falls the way it grows."
  5. The everyday refresh. A simple trim and blow-dry, for the many clients who are not looking for drama. "Not every visit is a makeover. Sometimes it is just good hair for the week."

Group 2: education

  1. How to ask for what you want. A clear reference photo next to the result. "Bring two photos: one you love and one you do not."
  2. Balayage, highlights or lowlights? A carousel with one real example per slide. "Same blonde, three ways to get there."
  3. Why colour takes so long. A time-lapse with each step labelled. "Four hours is not slow. Here is where the time goes."
  4. Keeping colour fresh between visits. One habit per post, given as your stylist's advice, not as a product promise. "Maya's one rule for keeping copper bright."
  5. The honest answer. A question clients ask at the basin. "Can you go from black to platinum in one visit? Our honest answer."

Group 3: the stylists

  1. Meet the stylist. A photo at her station, her specialty, what she loves doing. "This is Thu. Ask her for lived-in brunettes and soft layers."
  2. Who to book for what. A pinned carousel with each stylist and what they do best. "Curly cuts: Ana. Blonding: Maya. Short cuts and fades: Tom."
  3. A day at the chair. Short clips from first coffee to last blow-dry. "Tuesday with Ana: three curl cuts and one brave fringe."
  4. Training night. The team practising on mannequin heads after closing. "Learning a new colour technique, ready for you next month."
  5. The new face. A welcome post when someone joins, with her specialty and when she has openings. "Welcome, Linh. She has Saturday slots for new clients."

Group 4: booking and practical info

  1. The menu, explained. Services with "from" prices and how long each takes. "Prices depend on length and thickness. Ask us for a quote."
  2. The consultation. If you offer one, film what happens in those fifteen minutes. "Not sure what you want? Book a consultation first."
  3. Your first colour visit. What to expect, including any patch test your salon asks for. "First time colouring with us? Here is what happens before we start."
  4. Last-minute openings. A Story the minute someone cancels. "Ana has a 3pm cut today. Message us to take it."
  5. How to find us. The walk from the car park or the tram stop to your door. "Second floor, above the bakery. Lift on the left."

Only post what your salon really does. Rules on chemical services, patch tests and hygiene differ by country and region.

How to film a transformation that tells the truth

Transformations get saved and shared, and they are also where salons lose trust fastest. A filter that warms the colour brings in clients expecting a result you cannot repeat.

  • Pick one spot. A plain wall near a window, used every time. Daylight shows colour as it is.
  • Shoot the before first, before you wet or section the hair, and ask permission at that moment.
  • Show the back and the sides. Colour and cut live at the back of the head, where clients look first.
  • Add movement. Three seconds of her shaking her hair out shows a cut better than a photo.
  • No colour filters. Put the facts in the caption instead: the service, how long it took and who did it.

Where each post works best

Film once, then place the post where it fits.

  • Instagram is the portfolio new clients check before booking: transformations, stylist carousels, the pinned menu. Profile tools are at Instagram for Business.
  • TikTok shows short videos to people who do not follow you yet: reveals, time-lapses, honest answers. Setup is at TikTok for Business.
  • Facebook is where your neighbourhood and local groups are: hours, openings, hiring, team news. Page tools are at Meta for Business.

Booking buttons on business profiles depend on your country and booking system, and they change, so check inside the app.

Getting stylists involved without turning them into influencers

Many stylists already post on their own accounts. That helps the salon, as long as a few things are agreed.

  • One shared photo folder where stylists drop their best before and afters for the salon to use.
  • Tag both ways: the salon tags the stylist, the stylist tags the salon and its location.
  • One clip a week from each stylist, not a daily performance.
  • What happens when someone leaves. Who keeps the photos is easier to settle in writing on day one. Employment and image rights questions belong with a qualified adviser.

Asking clients for permission

Hair photos usually show a face, so the yes needs to be clear. Ask at the start, when you take the before photo, not after the blow-dry.

"I would love to post your colour on our Instagram when we are done. Is that okay? I can film just the back if you prefer."

  • Offer the back-of-the-head option. Many clients who would refuse a face say yes to that.
  • For anyone under 18, ask the parent. Ask whether she wants to be tagged.
  • Note who said yes, or add a tick box to your booking form. Privacy rules differ by country and tend to be stricter in Europe, so when in doubt, get the yes in writing.
  • If a client asks you to take a photo down, do it the same day.

Common mistakes

  • Only one hair type on the grid. Clients with curly, coily, fine or grey hair scroll past, assuming you are not for them.
  • Only dramatic makeovers. Most clients book trims, root touch-ups and blow-dries. Show those too.
  • Filtered colour. The client arrives expecting the filter, not the hair.
  • No stylist name. She loves the result but does not know who to book.
  • No prices, no way to book. She goes to the salon down the road that answers the question.
  • A different look on every channel. Different logos, colours and tone make a salon look less established than it is. Deciding the name, colours and voice once is brand work, and it is what we do at Bunchasake for owner-run businesses: see brand building for owner-run businesses.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a hair salon post on social media?

Three or four posts a week is enough, plus Stories when you have openings. Keeping that rhythm for months matters more than posting a lot for two weeks.

Should stylists post on their own accounts or on the salon's?

Both work. The salon account is the one you control, and stylist accounts add personality. Tag each other and share one photo folder.

Do we really need to show prices?

Showing "from" prices and what changes the final price avoids awkward surprises. If prices vary a lot, explain why and offer a consultation.

Can we repost a photo a client posted of her hair?

Ask her first, even if she tagged you, and credit her in the caption. Then note her yes with the others.
