Glossier: the beauty brand born from a blog and a community

Glossier did not start with a product line. It started with conversations with readers, then made exactly what those readers wanted.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Glossier had a community before it had a product: the Into The Gloss blog launched in 2010, the brand in 2014.
  • It listened to readers to decide what to make, so customers felt the brand was made for them.
  • A simple, consistent identity, led by its pink, lets customers recognise Glossier anywhere.
  • Every brand stalls at some point; Glossier added Sephora as a sales channel in 2023 but kept its own identity.
  • The lesson for beauty chains: talk to customers first, sell second, and keep every touchpoint in one voice.

In 2010 Emily Weiss, then working at Vogue, started the blog Into The Gloss. Its most popular feature was conversations with women about their skincare and makeup routines. Four years later, in October 2014, out of that same readership she launched Glossier with its first four products (Wikipedia).

Glossier's story is worth reading for owners of beauty chains, nail salons, spas or cosmetics brands, because it does one thing many brands skip: it listens to customers before it sells.

Community first, product second

Most brands make a product and then look for customers. Glossier went the other way. At launch it already had a loyal readership that had shared its routines, likes and frustrations. Product ideas came from those conversations. So customers did not feel sold to; they felt the brand was making something for them.

A simple identity anyone can recognise

Glossier chose a minimal style: clean packaging, clear type and, above all, a soft pink that runs through packaging, stores and displays. When it reached Sephora shelves, its displays kept that pink (Retail Dive). A consistent identity lets customers spot the brand even among hundreds of others.

Customers as storytellers

Instead of relying only on advertising, Glossier lets customers talk about it: real customer photos, real reviews, posts people share themselves. For a beauty brand, a recommendation from a friend carries more weight than any billboard.

Stalling, and moving on

No brand rises forever. Glossier cut staff in 2022 and changed its leadership. In 2023, after nearly a decade of selling only through its own channels, it launched in around 600 Sephora stores in the US and Canada (Retail Dive), followed by Sephora UK. It opened a new channel but kept its pink and its voice. That is how to grow without losing yourself.

Lessons for growing beauty chains

  • Talk to customers first. Ask what they need and like, and let the answers shape your services and products. This is the listening step in how we work.
  • Build a community, not just a customer list. Regulars sharing their experience are a beauty business's strongest channel. See how to build a community around a brand.
  • Choose one recognisable signal and keep it. One colour, one typeface, one photo style, repeated in every branch and every post.
  • Add channels, keep the voice. Marketplaces, malls or new branches: the brand must still speak in one voice.

Frequently asked questions

When was Glossier founded?

The brand launched in October 2014, growing out of the beauty blog Into The Gloss that Emily Weiss started in 2010.

When did Glossier start selling at Sephora?

In 2023, first in the US and Canada, then at Sephora UK.

Can a small beauty business learn from Glossier?

Yes. The biggest lesson is to listen to customers and let them tell your story. A small salon has the advantage of being closer to its customers than any big brand.
