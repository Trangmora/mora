Step 02, Positioning: know where you stand before you move

In the Positioning step Bunchasake analyses competitors in depth, identifies the customers who drive growth, then writes the positioning statement and brand story that every channel and every branch will tell.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Positioning answers four questions: who you are, who you are for, who you differ from and how.
  • We analyse competitors in depth: what they say, who they target, where they are strong and what gaps they leave.
  • You receive a positioning statement, brand story and key messages, always grounded in what the founder wants from step 01.
  • For companies with several product lines, this step also settles the brand architecture.

Positioning that worked in your first city may not hold in your fifth city, or in another country. As you grow, you meet new competitors, new customers and new price points. Ask ten employees how you differ from competitors. If you get ten different answers, it is time for Positioning.

What Bunchasake does in this step

  1. In-depth competitor analysis. What direct and indirect competitors say on their websites, social channels, packaging and stores; who they target, how they price, what customers praise and criticise.
  2. Finding the gap. What customers need that nobody says clearly or does well. That is usually where your brand has a natural advantage.
  3. Defining the customers who drive growth. Not everyone, but the people who will choose you, come back and recommend you.
  4. Writing the positioning statement. Three lines: who it is for, what we are, how we are different and why to believe it.
  5. Writing the brand story and key messages. Turning the Listening summary into a short story and a few messages every channel shares.

What you receive

  • A competitor analysis: strengths, weaknesses, how they talk and the gaps in the market.
  • A profile of your core customer groups.
  • A three-line positioning statement and the brand story.
  • Key messages per customer group; for multi-product companies, a brand architecture map.

Who is involved and what you approve

You and your brand lead review the analysis with us and add what only insiders know about competitors and customers. The final direction always rests on what the founder said in step 01. You approve the positioning and brand story before we move on to Identity.

For chains and growing brands

For chains entering a new market or Vietnamese brands going abroad, we analyse competitors in that market, not only at home. A famous name in Saigon can be a complete newcomer in Melbourne or Berlin, and needs a different way of telling its story.

How this step connects to the others

With positioning settled, step 03, Identity, turns it into visuals, colour, type and voice. Before it comes step 01, Listening.

Frequently asked questions

How is positioning different from a slogan?

A positioning statement is an internal compass that keeps product, pricing and communication decisions aligned. A slogan is one outward expression of it and can change with campaigns.

I already know my competitors. Why analyse them?

Insiders see direct competitors clearly but rarely map how they talk to customers and what gaps they leave. The analysis puts the whole team on the same picture.

What if we have several product lines?

This step settles the brand architecture: whether lines share the parent name or have their own, and how they relate, before anything is designed.
