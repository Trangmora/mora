Hire an agency, hire a marketer or use AI automation? No single option is right for every business. An agency suits campaigns and work that needs deep specialist skill. An employee suits high volume and work that needs someone on site. AI automation suits the work that repeats every day, such as writing posts, publishing and answering familiar questions. Most small businesses end up combining two. This guide puts the options side by side so you can decide for yourself, even when the right choice is not us.

Four ways a small business does marketing

  1. The owner does it. You take the photos, write the posts and answer messages yourself, usually in the evening.
  2. An employee. One person, or a small team, working full time for you alone.
  3. An agency or freelancer. An outside party takes the work on a monthly package or per project.
  4. AI automation. A system where AI does the repeated work and people approve. You run it yourself, or a team runs it with you. The idea is explained in our guide to what AI marketing automation is.

Cost: compare the kind of cost first, the number second

We do not quote "average" salaries or agency fees, because those figures vary enormously between cities and countries, and the numbers that circulate online usually have no clear source. A safer method is to ask for real quotes from two or three providers where you operate, then compare them with three questions.

  • Fixed or per job? A salary is fixed and is paid even in a slow month. A project fee stops when the work stops.
  • Paying for hours or for output? "Twenty posts a month" is clearer than "marketing support".
  • What are the hidden costs? The time you spend explaining, approving and correcting. Tool subscriptions. The time to hire again when someone leaves.

Sensible combinations

  • One employee with AI. AI handles the writing, images and scheduling. The employee puts their energy into what a machine cannot do: real filming, talking with customers, finding ideas in what happens in the shop.
  • An agency for campaigns, AI for the daily work. The agency creates the identity or the launch. After that the system keeps the channels running consistently in that identity.
  • The owner with a team running the AI. You hire nobody and only approve. This suits a business without enough work for a full-time marketer.

Whatever the combination, only one person should have the final say on voice and facts. When several people edit, nobody is responsible.

8 questions to ask before you sign with anyone

  1. Whose name are the accounts in? The page, the ad account, the domain and the Google profile must be in your name, with the other party only given access. You can set access in Meta's business tools and Google Business Profile.
  2. Who owns the content and images produced? Put it in the contract.
  3. Who actually works on my account every day? The person you meet at signing may not be the one doing the work.
  4. What is the approval process? At which step you approve, and how long you have.
  5. Do you use AI, and who checks before publishing? Using AI is not the problem. Nobody checking is.
  6. What is in the report, and how often?
  7. How much notice to stop, and what do I get back? Accounts, source files, the voice document, the content calendar.
  8. What do you not guarantee? An honest provider says plainly that they do not promise customer numbers or revenue.

These eight questions work for an agency, a freelancer, a candidate you plan to hire, and for us as well. If you are choosing a team abroad, see also our guide on how to choose an agency for international marketing.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the lowest price. Cheap is not cheap if you have to redo everything.
  • Hiring and walking away. No option works well without someone on your side supplying facts and approving.
  • Letting an outside party own the accounts. When you part ways, you lose the page.
  • Believing guaranteed numbers. Nobody controls the algorithm or a customer's decision.
  • Changing approach every three months. Every option needs time to learn your brand.

Frequently asked questions

Should a small business hire an agency or an in-house marketer?

It depends on the kind of work. Work that comes in bursts and needs specialist skill, such as a launch, paid ads or a brand identity, suits an agency. Daily, high-volume work that needs someone on site suits an employee. Repeated work such as writing and publishing posts can be carried by AI automation.

Can AI automation replace an agency?

Not entirely. It replaces the daily repeated work. Strategy, big ideas, filming and paid advertising still need people with the right expertise.

If I use AI automation, do I still need a marketing person?

You still need at least one person to supply correct information and approve. In a small shop that is usually the owner. In a chain, there should be one person in charge at head office.

How do I compare the cost of each option?

Collect real quotes where you operate, then compare three things: fixed or per job, paying for hours or for output, and the time you will spend managing. Do not rely on average figures with no clear source.

What must I keep whichever option I choose?

Ownership of the accounts and the domain, a person on your side who approves before publishing, and a document that records the brand's voice and facts so you depend on nobody.
