25 nail salon social media content ideas you can copy this week

You do not need a new idea every day. You need five content pillars, 25 posts to rotate and a weekly rhythm your team can keep.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • A nail salon needs five kinds of posts on repeat, which are the work, the transformation, the people, care and trust, and offers with practical info.
  • Three to five posts a week that you keep up all year are worth more than a busy fortnight followed by two silent months.
  • Write captions in the language most clients read, and add one short line in a second language only where it helps someone.
  • Always ask before you film a client's hands, and take the photo down the same day if she changes her mind.

The nail salon social media content ideas that keep working are not clever one-offs. They are five kinds of posts, repeated every week: the work, the transformation, the people, care and trust, and offers with practical info. Here are those five pillars, 25 posts to copy on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, a weekly rhythm in a table, and the words to ask a client before you film her hands.

The five content pillars every salon can use

A pillar is a topic you come back to. With five minutes between two clients, you only need to ask "which pillar is it today?"

  • The work. Finished sets, colours, shapes. This proves skill.
  • The transformation. Before and after, repairs, the process. This shows what one visit changes.
  • The people. Technicians, the owner, the regulars. Clients book a person, not a chair.
  • Care, hygiene and trust. Clean tools, honest advice, aftercare. This answers the worry nobody says out loud.
  • Offers and practical info. Prices, hours, parking, how to book. This turns a like into an appointment.

Most salons post only the first pillar. A new client needs all five.

25 nail salon social media content ideas, grouped by pillar

Each idea comes with one line you can copy. Change the names, colours and times to your own.

Pillar 1: the work

  1. Set of the day. One finished set by the window, hand on a plain towel. "Milky pink almond, done by Trang this morning."
  2. Colour of the week. Three bottles in a row, one swatch each. "Three reds for December. Which one is yours?"
  3. Shape guide. Five sample tips side by side, from square to coffin. "Not sure what to ask for? Save this."
  4. Seasonal design menu. Four designs for the coming holiday, each with a name and a starting price. "Holiday sets from $55. Book by the 20th."
  5. The pedicure. Feet on a clean towel after a spa pedicure, a post many salons forget. "Sandal season starts here."

Pillar 2: the transformation

  1. Before and after. Same hand, same angle, same light. "Six weeks of grow-out, fresh again in under an hour."
  2. The broken nail. A short clip from snapped to fixed. "Broke one before the weekend? We repair single nails."
  3. Gentle removal. Film the soak-off, with no picking and no rough filing. "Please do not peel your gel. Let us take it off."
  4. Start to finish. A 15-second time-lapse from prep to top coat. "What 60 minutes in our chair looks like."
  5. From photo to nails. The picture a client brought in, then your version. "She showed us this. Here is what she left with."

Pillar 3: the people

  1. Meet the technician. A photo at her station, her name, what she does best. "This is Linh. Ask her for French tips. She speaks English and Vietnamese."
  2. Why we opened. The owner's story in four sentences. "My aunt taught me nails at her kitchen table. This salon carries her name."
  3. Opening up. Lights on, towels folded, first coffee. "8:45, fifteen minutes before the first client."
  4. A regular's moment. A birthday set, or a bride with her bridesmaids, with their yes. "Congratulations, Sarah. Thank you for letting us be part of it."
  5. Practice night. A technician learning a new technique after closing. "Tuesday, 7pm. Practising chrome so it is ready for you."

Pillar 4: care, hygiene and trust

  1. How we clean our tools. Film the real steps, from washing to opening a sealed pouch at the table. "This is what happens to our tools after your appointment."
  2. The pedicure chair. The basin being cleaned between two clients. "Every time, before you sit down."
  3. One aftercare tip. A single habit per post. "Cuticle oil every night. Gloves for the dishes."
  4. An honest answer. One question clients ask at the desk. "How long should gel last? Here is our honest answer."
  5. When we say no. Explain kindly why you will not work on a nail that looks infected, and that a doctor should see it first. "We would rather lose a booking than hurt your nail."

Only show what you really do. Hygiene rules differ by state and country, so never promise more than happens in the salon.

Pillar 5: offers and practical info

  1. The price list. A clear, readable menu with how to book, pinned to the top of each page. "Our prices, updated for this season."
  2. Last-minute opening. Post it as soon as someone cancels. "2:30 today just opened with Mai. Message us to take it."
  3. Holiday hours. A week before every holiday. "Closed on the 25th, open 10 to 4 on the 26th."
  4. How to find us. Film the walk from the car park to your door. "We are between the pharmacy and the bakery. Free parking in front."
  5. An offer with an end date. One offer for your quiet days, not a permanent discount. "Mondays and Tuesdays in January, two free accent nails with any gel set."

What goes on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok

Film once, then place the post where it fits.

  • Facebook is the notice board: hours, offers, hiring, reviews and community news. Many neighbourhood and community groups are here. Page tools are explained at Meta for Business.
  • Instagram is the portfolio new clients often check before they book. Profile setup, Reels, carousels and Stories have their own guide: Instagram content ideas for nail salons.
  • TikTok is short video, shown to people who do not follow you yet. Process clips, repairs and personality fit here. Account setup is covered at TikTok for Business.

How to write captions, with a second-language line where it helps

Most of your clients read English, so English goes first. Write the way you speak at the front desk, in three short lines: what it is, who did it, how to book.

Short almond, milky pink gel. Done by Trang.

Book with the link in our profile or call the salon.

Tiệm mở cửa cả cuối tuần, nhận khách hẹn và khách walk-in.

Add a line in a second language when many of your regulars speak it, for example Vietnamese or Spanish: holiday hours around a festival such as Lunar New Year, a hiring post, a greeting to your regulars. Keep it to one line under the English, not a full translation. Write prices and times as numbers, and ask someone fluent to read the English before you post. How to ask clients for permission to post their hands

Ask before you film, not after. The easiest moment is when the top coat is done and she is admiring the result.

"These came out so well. May I take a photo of your hands for our Instagram and Facebook? Just hands, no face."

In her own language, if she prefers it: "Em chụp bộ móng đăng lên trang của tiệm được không chị? Chỉ chụp tay thôi, không chụp mặt."

  • If she says no, thank her and move on.
  • Faces, children, tattoos and anything else that identifies her need a clear yes. For anyone under 18, ask the parent.
  • Ask whether she wants to be tagged.
  • Keep a note of who said yes, or add a tick box to your booking form. Privacy rules differ by country and tend to be stricter in Europe, so when in doubt, get the yes in writing.
  • If a client later asks you to remove a photo, remove it the same day.

Common mistakes

  • Only finished nails. That is one pillar out of five. Nobody learns who you are or what it costs.
  • Photos that are not yours. A design saved from the internet is someone else's work, and the client will expect exactly that set.
  • No price, no hours, no way to book. A beautiful post that leads nowhere.
  • A different look on every page. Three logos and four colour schemes make people wonder whether it is the same salon. Deciding the name, colours and voice once is brand work, and it is the work we do at Bunchasake for owner-run businesses: see brand and marketing for nail salons.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a nail salon post on social media?

Three to five posts a week is enough, plus Stories on days when you have openings. What matters more than the number is how many weeks in a row you keep it up.

Which platform should we start with?

Start where your clients already are. If you are not sure, use Facebook for practical information and Instagram for your work, then add TikTok later. One page done well is better than three abandoned.

Do we need permission if only the hands are visible?

Yes, always ask. It is polite, and it is safer for the salon because the rules are not the same everywhere.

What if nobody on the team likes being on camera?

You do not need faces. Hands, tools, bottles and the salon itself carry most posts.
