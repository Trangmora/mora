A holiday content plan should be made now, not when the shop is already fully booked. From early December to mid-February there are eleven of the busiest weeks of the year, with three occasions in a row: Christmas, New Year, and Tet, the Lunar New Year, which falls on Saturday 6 February 2027. It is also when owners have the least time to think about content. The method in this guide: sit down once and plan the whole season, let AI draft everything that can be drafted, then each week you only approve and add real photos.

Why the holiday content plan has to start now

  • Customers book and order early. People sort out gifts, beauty appointments and party plans weeks before the day.
  • You will not have time. The peak week is the week when nobody in the shop is free to write a caption.
  • Facts drift most easily in this season. Hours change, there are closed days and a last booking date. One mistake and a customer arrives at a locked door.

Eleven weeks sounds long, but it is really only three waves of content: before and during Christmas, around New Year, and the Tet season.

First job: fix the dates and hours, then write

Before thinking about posts, fill in this table. Every piece of content for the season reads from it.

Once it is filled in, update the special hours on your Google profile straight away. How to set holiday hours is in the Google Business Profile Help Center. If you have a chatbot answering messages, add this table to its knowledge document, as our guide to the AI chatbot for customer messages explains.

One main message a week, repeated in three or four formats: an image post, a short video, a story, a pinned line. Customers do not read every post you publish, so repetition is necessary. How to build the day-by-day calendar is in our guide to the AI content calendar for small business.

Give AI the fixed table of dates together with your voice document, and ask it to draft the whole season in one go. If you have no voice document yet, follow our guide on teaching AI your brand voice. An AI-generated illustration is fine for a greeting card. Pictures of products and services must be real.

Three notice templates to copy

TypeTemplate
Closure"We are closed for Tet from [date] to [date] and reopen on [date]. Our last day for bookings is [date]. If you need an appointment before Tet, message us soon."
Slots left"This week we still have [day, time]. After [date] we are fully booked until Tet. Message us to hold a slot."
Reopening"We reopen on [date], with our usual hours. Wishing you a healthy new year. The new year's calendar is open, so message us to book."

These three notices should be pinned to the top of your page, set as the automatic reply and posted on the door. The same wording, the same dates and times, everywhere.

If your business is abroad

  • Christmas and New Year are the main season. Local customers book, buy gifts and plan parties around these two. Write for them in their language.
  • Tet belongs to the community. In many places Tet is not a public holiday, so the shop stays open. A greeting in Vietnamese for Vietnamese customers, with one short line of explanation for local customers, is usually received very warmly.
  • Tell a story, do not only send wishes. A dish cooked only at Tet, a custom in your family. Local customers are curious about these things.

How to use two languages in one post is in our guide to bilingual marketing for small businesses.

The checklist before each occasion

  1. Special hours are updated on the Google profile.
  2. The closure notice is pinned on Facebook and Instagram.
  3. The automatic reply and the chatbot have the new hours.
  4. The website and booking page have the closed days blocked.
  5. Posts are scheduled for your busiest days. Scheduling is available in Meta's business tools.
  6. One person is responsible for reading messages during the closure, or the away message says clearly when you will reply.
  7. The reopening notice is written and scheduled.

Common mistakes

  • Announcing the closure too late. Customers have made plans before they learn you are closed.
  • Different hours on each channel. Fixed on Facebook, forgotten on Google.
  • "Almost fully booked" when the calendar is open. Customers notice, and next time they do not believe you.
  • AI images for the Tet dish or the Tet nail set. Customers arrive and see something different.
  • A burst of posts, then silence. After Tet is exactly when customers need to know you have reopened.
  • Wishes for the wrong occasion or culture. Anything AI writes about customs must be reread by someone who knows that culture.

Frequently asked questions

When should Tet content start?

About four to five weeks before Tet, which means early January for Tet on 6 February 2027. The closure notice and last booking date should be out at least two weeks before you close.

Can AI draft content for the whole holiday season in advance?

Yes, if you give it the fixed table of dates and your voice document. It drafts captions, notices and greetings. Real photos, offers and available slots are still supplied and decided by you.

How many posts a week during the holidays?

No more than usual. What matters is one main message each week, repeated in a few formats. In the week you announce the closure, repeat it two or three times.

What happens to customer messages while we are closed?

Set an away message that says until when you are closed and when you will reply. If you have a chatbot, add the closure to its knowledge document so it answers correctly and records booking requests for the reopening day.

Should we use AI-generated images for Tet greetings?

Greeting cards and decorative images are fine. Pictures of products, dishes or services should be your own real photos, because customers will compare them with what they receive.
