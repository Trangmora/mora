How to reach Vietnamese customers overseas, a plan for owners who are part of the community

Vietnamese customers abroad rarely find a business through an advert. They find it through people and places they already trust. This guide shows where those are and how to show up there properly.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Vietnamese customers overseas are reached through trust, so show up where the community already gathers instead of trying to pull it somewhere new.
  • The community is not one audience. Elders, working families, students and the generation raised abroad each use different channels.
  • In a Facebook group, be a useful member first. One helpful post that follows the rules is worth more than ten adverts that get deleted.
  • Plan around the community calendar. Tết, Vu Lan and Trung thu are the weeks when Vietnamese customers are looking for you.
  • Word of mouth grows faster when you give customers one clear sentence to repeat and an easy way to pass on your number.

If you want to know how to reach Vietnamese customers overseas, start with one idea: the community moves on trust, and trust travels through people and places, not through adverts. The practical work is to be present where Vietnamese people near you already gather, online and in person, to be easy to contact in Vietnamese, and to give happy customers something simple to repeat. This guide walks through each part, with a table and a 30-day checklist you can copy.

How the community finds a business

Think about how you found your own dentist, accountant or hairdresser after you moved abroad. Most likely someone told you. A cousin, a friend from church, a woman at the Asian supermarket, a post in a Facebook group where somebody asked "Có ai biết chỗ nào làm tóc người Việt không?"

That is still how it works. A recommendation passes along a short chain: someone asks, someone answers with a name, and the person who asked looks the name up to check that it is real. Your job has two parts. Be the name that gets mentioned, and look trustworthy when someone checks.

Decide which Vietnamese customers you mean

"The Vietnamese community" is at least four groups, and they do not use the same channels.

  • Elders and long-settled families. They phone instead of messaging, read community newspapers, listen to Vietnamese radio where it exists, and meet at the temple, the church and the market. They value being greeted properly and spoken to in Vietnamese.
  • Working families. Busy, practical, active in Facebook groups and group chats. They ask for recommendations in public and book by message.
  • Students and recent arrivals. New to the city, short on contacts, and looking for a taste of home. They live in student groups and chat apps, and they share finds quickly.
  • The generation raised abroad. Comfortable in the local language, on Instagram and TikTok, and often the ones who choose where the whole family eats or who does grandma's nails before a wedding.

Pick the one or two groups that matter most to your trade and start there. For the question of which language to use with each group, see our guide to bilingual marketing for Vietnamese businesses abroad.

How to post in Vietnamese Facebook groups without being removed

Community groups are run by volunteers who are tired of adverts. Read the pinned rules before you write anything. Many groups allow business posts only on certain days or in a certain thread, and some do not allow them at all. Rules differ from group to group and change over time, so check each one.

Then behave like a neighbour.

  1. Help first. When someone asks where to buy rice paper or how to renew a document, answer if you know. People notice names that keep being useful.
  2. Introduce yourself once, as a person. Say who you are, where you are from, what you opened and why.
  3. Post news, not slogans. A new dish, Tết opening hours, a job opening, a free tea for students during exam week.

An introduction that works reads something like this:

Chào cả nhà, em là Hạnh, quê Hải Phòng, qua đây được tám năm. Vợ chồng em mới mở quán bánh đa cua nhỏ ở gần ga trung tâm, nấu đúng kiểu Hải Phòng. Quán mở từ thứ Ba đến Chủ nhật. Cả nhà có dịp ghé thì cho em xin góp ý nha. Em cảm ơn admin đã cho em đăng bài.

In English: a name, a hometown, what the couple opened and where, a request for feedback and a thank you to the admin. No price war, no "best in town". If you run a page as well as a personal profile, Meta for Business explains the tools available for pages.

Be easy to reach in Vietnamese

Many Vietnamese customers will not fill in a booking form. They will send a message that says "Chị ơi mai còn chỗ không?" and wait. If the answer comes quickly and warmly, you have a customer.

  • Choose the chat apps your community uses. Zalo is common among people with family in Vietnam. Messenger and WhatsApp are common almost everywhere. Ask ten customers which one they open first.
  • Use a business number, not your personal one, so staff can share the work. WhatsApp Business lets a small business set up a profile and saved replies. Features vary by country and change, so look at what is offered where you are.
  • Write five saved replies in Vietnamese: opening hours, address and parking, prices or menu, how to book, and what to do for a large group.

Plan around the community calendar

Demand in the community rises at predictable moments. Mark these and prepare two to three weeks ahead. Dates follow the lunar calendar, so check them each year.

  • Tết. The busiest season for food, gifts, hair, nails and anything to do with looking good and eating together. Announce opening hours and pre-orders early.
  • Rằm tháng Giêng and Vu Lan. Vegetarian dishes, offerings, flowers, family meals.
  • Trung thu. Mooncakes, lanterns, children's events.

Make word of mouth easy

People recommend what they can describe in one sentence. Give them that sentence. "The quán near the station where the owner is from Hải Phòng." "The salon where they speak Vietnamese and never rush you." If you are not sure what your sentence is, our guide on how Vietnamese business owners abroad can build a brand starts there. Then put your chat number on a card that customers can hand to a friend.

How to reach Vietnamese customers overseas in your first 30 days

  • Week 1: Choose your one or two customer groups. Claim and correct your Google Business Profile so people who check your name find real information.
  • Week 1: Set up one business chat number and write five saved replies in Vietnamese.
  • Week 2: Join the two or three most active local Vietnamese groups. Read the rules. Answer three questions before you post anything about yourself.
  • Week 3: Post your introduction where the rules allow. Reply to every comment.
  • Week 3: Contact one temple, church, school or association and ask what they need for their next event.
  • Week 4: Mark the next community date on your calendar and plan one offer for it.
  • Week 4: Ask ten customers how they heard about you, and write the answers down.

Common mistakes

  • Spamming groups. The same advert in ten groups on the same day gets you removed and remembered for the wrong reason.
  • Competing only on price. A discount brings people once. In a small community, undercutting neighbours also costs you goodwill you will need later.
  • Arguing in public. One heated reply to a complaint is screenshotted and passed around. Answer calmly, then move the conversation to a private message.
  • Counting on the community alone. In most cities it is not large enough to fill a business every day. Local customers need attention too.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to advertise or to rely on word of mouth?

For the Vietnamese community, start with word of mouth and presence, because that is how people decide. Paid adverts can add reach later, but they work poorly if nobody in the community has heard of you or if your listing looks empty when people check.

Which chat app should a Vietnamese business abroad use?

Use the one your customers already open every day. Ask them. In many places that means Zalo for people with close ties to Vietnam and Messenger or WhatsApp for everyone else. One number that is always answered is better than three that are not.

There are very few Vietnamese people in my town. Is this still worth doing?

Yes, on a smaller scale. A small community is tightly connected, so one good impression travels far. Keep the chat number, the saved replies and the calendar, and put most of your remaining effort into local customers.

How do I know which channel is working?

Ask every new customer how they heard about you and keep a tally on paper at the counter. After a month the pattern is usually clear, and you can drop the channel nobody mentions.
