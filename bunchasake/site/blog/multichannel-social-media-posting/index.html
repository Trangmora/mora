Facebook Instagram TikTok automation: multichannel posting from one plan

Plan one idea, film it once, write three short versions and schedule them ahead. Automation handles the timing. A person still approves every post and answers every reply. Here is the routine.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Multichannel posting works when one idea is planned once and adapted for each channel, not copied word for word to all three.
  • Automation is good at timing and reminders, while filming, approving and replying stay with people.
  • Film vertically, keep a clean copy with no text or logo on it, and you can cut the same clip for Facebook, Instagram and TikTok.
  • Scheduling features differ between platforms and change over time, so check each platform's official pages before you build a routine around one feature.
  • A small business does better on two channels it answers every day than on three it has left on autopilot.

Facebook Instagram TikTok automation, for a small business, means one plan that feeds three channels. You decide the idea once, film it once, write a short version for each platform and schedule all three ahead of time. The software takes care of the clock. People still do the three things that matter: making something real, approving it, and answering the customers who reply. This guide gives you the plan sheet, the adaptation rules and a weekly routine of about an hour.

What Facebook Instagram TikTok automation can and cannot do

It helps to be exact about the word, because "automation" promises more than it delivers.

What can be automated:

  • Publishing an approved post at a chosen date and time.
  • Reminding the right person that a post needs filming or approval.
  • Producing a first draft of each caption from one idea, if you use an AI assistant.
  • Keeping all three channels in one calendar, so you can see gaps at a glance.

What cannot:

  • Filming your real work, your real food and your real team.
  • Deciding whether a post is true, kind and on time.
  • Replying to comments and messages in a way that makes a customer feel seen.

So the honest picture is this: automation removes the daily "what do we post, and when" from your day. It does not remove you.

Film once, cut three ways

Most of the time saved in multichannel posting is saved at the filming stage. A few habits make one clip usable everywhere.

  1. Film vertically. All three channels show vertical video well. Horizontal clips leave empty space.
  2. Keep a clean original. Save the clip from your phone camera with no text, music or logo on it. Add those separately for each channel. A video downloaded from one app and posted on another carries that app's logo and looks second-hand.
  3. Leave room at the edges. Each app covers part of the screen with buttons and captions. Keep faces, hands and text near the middle.
  4. Show the result first. The finished nails or the steaming bowl go in the first seconds, the process after.
  5. Take one still photo too. Facebook and your Google listing can use a photo when a video is too much.
  6. Put the words on screen. Many people watch without sound. A short line of text does the talking.

How to adapt one idea into three captions

Take the row from the sheet: bún bò Huế, weekends only. The fact is the same. The telling changes.

Facebook: "Bún bò Huế is back on weekends. My mother starts the broth on Friday night with lemongrass and beef shank, the way she learned it at home. Saturday and Sunday from 11 a.m. until the pot is empty. Parking is behind the building. Cuối tuần này quán có bún bò Huế, mời cả nhà ghé."

Instagram: "Weekends only: bún bò Huế. Lemongrass, slow broth, a little heat. Save this for Saturday."

TikTok: text on screen, "What my mom cooks when she misses Huế". Caption: "Bún bò Huế, weekends only, at [restaurant name] in [city]."

Three rules sit behind those versions:

  • Change the first line. Facebook opens with the news, Instagram with the craving, TikTok with a small story.
  • Change the next step. "Link in bio" means nothing on Facebook. "Call us" is rarely what a TikTok viewer does.
  • Keep the facts identical. Days, hours and names must match on all three, or customers will trust none of them.

An AI assistant can draft the three versions from one sentence. A person still reads them. Our guide to an AI marketing assistant for small business shows how to ask.

Scheduling: what to check before you rely on it

There are two ways to schedule. You can use the scheduling features inside each platform, or one scheduling tool that connects to several accounts. Either can work. We do not recommend a product here, because the right choice depends on your accounts and it changes.

Before you build a routine, check these points on the official pages: Meta's business tools for Facebook and Instagram, Instagram for Business and TikTok for Business.

  • Whether your account type allows scheduling at all. A business or professional account is often required.
  • Which formats can be scheduled. Posts and short videos often can, while some formats may still need to be published by hand.
  • Whether features such as popular sounds are available on scheduled posts. Often they are only available when you post inside the app.
  • Who on your team has access, and how to remove a person who leaves.

Do not publish all three at the same minute. Spreading one idea over two days gives regulars who follow you everywhere something fresh each time, and gives you time to answer.

A weekly routine of about an hour

  1. Pick three ideas from the month's plan for the coming week.
  2. Film them in one session, on a quiet morning, following the six habits above.
  3. Draft the three versions of each idea.
  4. Approve. One person reads every caption out loud, checks every fact and gives the final yes.
  5. Schedule and mark the status in the sheet.
  6. Answer. Each day, the named person reads comments and messages on every channel.

The last step is the one that brings bookings. Simple repeated questions can get a prepared answer. Complaints, prices and anything about health or safety always get a reply from a person. Our guide to social media inbox automation explains where to draw that line.

Common mistakes

  • Copying one caption to all three. It reads as a broadcast, and the wrong next step confuses people.
  • Reposting a clip with another app's logo on it. Always upload the clean original.
  • Scheduling a month and disappearing. A scheduled post that nobody answers tells customers the lights are on and nobody is home.
  • Running three channels because you feel you should. If your team can answer only two each day, run two well.
  • Forgetting to pause. When something serious happens in your town or your business, stop the schedule and post by hand.

Frequently asked questions

Can I post the same video to Facebook, Instagram and TikTok?

Yes, if you upload the clean original to each one and write a caption that fits the channel. Avoid downloading the video from one app to post it on another, because it will carry that app's logo.

Do I need to be on all three channels?

No. Choose the channels where your customers already are and where your team can answer every day. Two channels done well are better than three left unattended.

Should all three posts go out at the same time?

It is better to spread them over a day or two. Customers who follow you on more than one channel see something new each time, and your team has time to reply.

Can everything be scheduled in advance?

Not always. What can be scheduled depends on the platform, the format and your account type, and it changes, so check the official pages and expect to publish some things by hand.
