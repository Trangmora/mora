Beauty salon marketing ideas for lash, brow, waxing and makeup studios

The beauty salon marketing that works is local and repeatable: be easy to find, show real work, make booking simple and give happy clients a reason to return and bring a friend. Pick a few ideas below and run them every week.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Lash, brow and waxing clients return on a natural cycle, so rebooking before they leave is the cheapest marketing a beauty studio has.
  • Name services the way clients search for them, such as lash lift or brow lamination, on your menu, booking page and Google listing.
  • Content that calms a nervous first-timer brings bookings that polished result photos alone do not.
  • Local businesses whose clients have an event coming up, such as bridal shops and photographers, are a steady, low-cost source of new clients.
  • Ask every happy client for a review, never pay for one, and get permission before you post anyone's photo.

The best beauty salon marketing ideas cost little, work in your own neighbourhood and can be repeated every week without a marketing team. For a lash, brow, waxing or makeup studio, that means: be found for the exact service people search for, show real work, calm first-timers and bring every client back on their natural cycle. Below are fourteen ideas in that order, a four-week plan you can copy and the mistakes worth avoiding.

Everything here is do-it-yourself. Pick three or four ideas that suit your studio rather than all fourteen at once.

Why a beauty studio markets differently

  • Clients return on a cycle. Lash fills, brow tidies and waxing all have a natural next visit. Your best marketing is the booking made before the client leaves.
  • First-timers are often nervous. A first wax or a first set of lashes raises questions. The studio that answers them clearly wins the booking.
  • Makeup follows events. Weddings, graduations and parties create demand on dates you can see coming.
  • Clients choose the artist. People follow a person whose work they like, so show your team, not only the results.

Be found for the service people search for

1. Make your Google listing match what you actually do

Claim or update your Google Business Profile. Choose categories that fit, add each service by its plain name with a price or "from" price, post real photos of the room and the work, and keep holiday hours correct. Someone searching for brow lamination nearby should see at once that you do it.

2. Use the words clients use

Lash lift, brow lamination, Brazilian wax, bridal makeup trial. Keep a signature name if you like it, but put the plain name beside it on your menu, booking system and listings. Nobody searches for a name they have never heard.

3. Put one booking link everywhere

Instagram bio, Google listing, website, the end of every message, and a QR code on the desk and the aftercare card. Every extra step between interest and a booked time loses people.

Show real work and the people behind it

4. Post results the honest way

Fresh results in the same light and from the same angle, always with the client's permission. When a client agrees, add a photo from a later visit, because it shows how the work wears. Caption example: "Lash lift and tint, no extensions. Book with the link in our bio." If many clients read Vietnamese or another language, add a second line: "Uốn mi và nhuộm mi, không nối mi. Đặt lịch qua link ở bio."

5. Calm the first-timer

Turn the questions people ask at the desk into short posts and a saved "First visit" highlight: what happens at a first wax, how long a lash appointment takes, what a patch test is, how to prepare. Base any aftercare advice on the instructions for the products you use. A short video of the room and the artist saying hello calms nerves better than any discount.

6. Introduce each artist

One post per team member: name, favourite service, a few photos of their work and how to book with them. For more post formats, our salon social media content ideas adapt easily to lash, brow and makeup work.

Bring clients back on their natural cycle

7. Rebook before they leave

At the end of each appointment, the artist suggests when the next visit makes sense and offers a time: "Your next fill will be due in a few weeks. Shall I hold Thursday the 14th at 5 pm?" It costs nothing and fills next month's diary today.

8. Send one reminder when the next visit is due

If a client did not rebook, one friendly message around the due date is helpful, not pushy. Use your booking system or WhatsApp Business, and only message people who agreed to hear from you, because rules on marketing messages differ by country. Example: "Hi Mai, it's been a while since your brow tidy. We have time on Saturday morning if that suits. Reply here or book with the link."

9. A simple loyalty card

A stamp card or a note in the booking system: after a set number of visits, a free add-on such as a brow tint. Reward the habit you want, which is coming back on time.

10. An aftercare card that sells the next visit

A small card in your brand colours with aftercare steps, the artist's name, the suggested date for the next visit and a QR code to book. Clients keep it because it is useful, and it travels in their bag with your name on it.

Local beauty salon marketing ideas that cost little

11. Partner with businesses whose clients have an event coming

Bridal shops, wedding photographers, dress hire, hair salons that do not offer makeup. Bring a short printed menu and a few photos, offer their clients a clear thank-you such as a free lash tint with a bridal booking, and recommend them to your clients in return.

12. Packages around the calendar

Plan around dates your clients already care about: wedding season, graduations, holiday parties, summer trips. "Holiday ready: brows, lash lift and leg wax in one visit" is easier to choose than three separate bookings.

13. Bring-a-friend pairs and small groups

Two brow appointments side by side, or a small makeup lesson for friends before a party. Groups bring in people who would not have booked alone.

14. Ask for reviews at the right moment

Ask when the client is looking in the mirror and smiling, then send the review link in your thank-you message. Never pay for reviews, never trade a discount for a good one, and ask every client, not only the ones you expect to be happy. The FTC's business guidance explains the US rules on reviews; other countries have their own. Reply to every one; our review response examples work for any beauty service.

When paid ads make sense

To be plain: Bunchasake does not run ads, so this part is up to you. Wait until your listing, booking link and recent photos are in order and you have open slots to fill. Then test small: one service, one nearby area, a spending limit you are comfortable losing, and a way to count the bookings that came from the ad. Platform options change often, so follow what your own account shows. Our salon advertising ideas walk through a small test step by step.

Common mistakes

  • Deep first-visit discounts. They attract bargain hunters who rarely return at full price.
  • Only perfect results, no explanation. Say what the service is, how long it takes and what it costs.
  • Stock photos or another studio's work. Clients notice when the room and results do not match.
  • Letting clients leave without a next date. The cycle is your advantage; use it.
  • Promises you cannot keep. "Painless", "suits everyone" or "lasts forever" set expectations no service can meet.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to market a lash or brow studio?

Rebooking at the end of every appointment, an accurate Google listing with plain service names, and asking every happy client for a review. All three are free and build on each other.

Should I offer a discount on the first visit?

A small welcome is fine, but a free add-on such as a tint usually works better than a deep discount. It rewards the visit without teaching clients that your real price is lower.

How often should a beauty salon post on social media?

As often as you can keep up for months, not weeks. A steady rhythm, such as three posts a week, beats a burst of daily posts followed by silence.

Can I post photos of my clients?

Only with their permission, ideally recorded in writing or in your booking system. Privacy rules differ by country, so ask a qualified adviser if you are unsure, and never post someone who said no.

Which idea should I start with this week?

Rebooking and your Google listing. They cost nothing and help every other idea work.
