How to build an e-commerce brand, not just an online store

An online store sells a product once. An e-commerce brand gives people a reason to choose you, come back and tell a friend. Start with one clear position, then make product pages, the parcel, reviews and email say the same thing.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • An online store is a place to pay. An e-commerce brand is a reason to choose you over a cheaper store with a similar product.
  • Write your position in one sentence before you choose a theme, a logo or an ad budget.
  • A product page does the work of a good shop assistant, so it needs answers to real questions, not more adjectives.
  • The parcel is the only moment an online brand is held in the hand. Plan what the customer sees, reads and feels when they open it.
  • Reviews and email turn a first order into a habit. Ask honestly, reply to every review and write emails the way you talk.

Anyone can open an online store in an afternoon. Building a brand around it takes longer, because a brand is what makes a stranger choose your store over a cheaper one with a similar product, and then come back without being reminded. If you want to know how to build an e-commerce brand, work in five layers: a clear position, product pages that answer questions, a parcel worth opening, honest reviews and email that sounds like a person.

This works the same for a candle maker selling from her kitchen, a family selling sauces or a small clothing label, on your own website, a marketplace or both. If you also have a physical shop, our guide to retail store branding covers the street side.

A store sells a product, a brand gives a reason

Picture two sellers of handmade ceramic mugs. The first lists "Ceramic mug, 350 ml, blue" on a white background. The second shows the same mug on a kitchen table in morning light: "For the first coffee of a slow morning. Thrown by hand in our studio, so no two are alike." Same clay, similar price. Only one gives the customer something to feel and something to repeat to a friend.

Online, a competitor is always one click away. If all you offer is the product, you end up competing on price. A brand gives other reasons to stay: people trust you, like how you talk and know what they will get.

Start with one sentence of positioning

Before you choose a store theme or pay for a logo, write one sentence:

For [who], [brand] is the [what] that [main difference], because [proof].

Example: "For people who cook at home on busy weeknights, our sauces make one pan taste like a family kitchen, because every recipe comes from our mother's notebook."

  • Ask real customers. Message five buyers and ask why they chose you. Use their words.
  • Decide what you are not. Not the cheapest, not everything for everyone.
  • Check the proof. If you cannot show it with a photo, a process or a review, take it out.

Product pages that work like a good shop assistant

In a shop, a customer can pick the product up, try it and ask someone. Online, the product page does all of that. Collect the questions people ask in messages and at markets, such as how big it really is, what it is made of, whether it will suit them and what happens if it does not, and answer them on the page. A simple order:

  1. A name that says what it is. "Chilli garlic oil, medium hot" before any poetic name.
  2. Photos in a fixed set. The product alone, in use, next to a hand for scale, a close detail and the packaging.
  3. Two or three sentences of story. Why this product exists and who made it.
  4. The facts in a short list. Size, materials or ingredients, care, what is in the box.
  5. Delivery and returns next to the buy button, not hidden in the footer.
  6. Reviews, with the newest easy to see.

Before: "Premium quality scented candle, perfect gift, amazing fragrance." After: "Fig and black tea, poured by hand in small batches. The glass jar can be reused for pens or herbs." The second answers questions. The first only stacks adjectives.

How to build an e-commerce brand that people can find

One thing to say plainly: Bunchasake does not run ads or sell SEO as a service. This section is for doing it yourself, and since platforms change their features often, follow what your own dashboard shows.

  • Use the customer's words. Name products the way people search for them, not with internal codes.
  • Write your own descriptions. Copied supplier text and photos make you look like every other store selling that item. The basics of how pages get found are in Google's SEO Starter Guide.
  • Marketplaces have their own search. If you sell on Etsy or similar, read its seller resources, such as the Etsy Seller Handbook, and keep the same name, logo and voice there.
  • Ads come after the page works. If friends and past customers visit a product page and do not buy, ads only send more people to a page that does not persuade. When it does sell, test small: one product, one audience, a budget you can afford to lose and a result decided in advance.

The parcel: the only moment your brand is held in the hand

A physical shop has a sign and a greeting. An online store has the moment a parcel is opened. Plan it like a small scene:

  1. The outside. A plain mailer with a logo sticker or stamp is enough.
  2. The first thing they see. A card on top, the product facing up, nothing rattling around.
  3. The card. A short thank you, one line of your story, care tips and how to reach you.
  4. The protection. A broken item is the worst branding you can ship.

Pin a photo of a correctly packed order above the packing table so anyone can repeat it. Our guide to packaging design for small brands goes deeper into materials and labels.

Reviews: proof from strangers

  • Ask at the right time. After the product has arrived and been used.
  • Ask everyone, not only happy customers. Picking who you ask so only good reviews appear gives a false picture.
  • Never buy, write or reward reviews. Many countries have rules on reviews and endorsements. In the United States, the FTC's business guidance is the official place to read about them; elsewhere, ask a qualified adviser.
  • Reply to every review. Thank people by name. For a bad one, apologise, say what you will do and continue in a private message.
  • Read them for words. When three customers describe your product the same way, put that phrase on the product page.

Email: the channel you own

Social platforms change what they show, and accounts can be lost. A list of customers who agreed to hear from you is yours. Ask for permission clearly; privacy rules differ by country. Set up four emails first:

  1. Order confirmation, in your voice, with what happens next.
  2. Shipping: when it arrives and what to do if it does not.
  3. A check-in after delivery: care tips and a simple request for a review.
  4. A welcome for subscribers: your story and what emails to expect.

After that, one letter a month is enough: a new product, a look behind the scenes, a customer photo shared with permission. If every email is a discount, people learn to wait for the next one.

Common mistakes

  • Paying for ads before the product page works. You pay to send people to a page that does not persuade.
  • Copying the supplier's photos and text. Only price is left to compete on.
  • A beautiful logo and a confusing returns policy. Clear answers build more trust than design.
  • A different look on every channel. Customers do not connect the marketplace, Instagram and the parcel.
  • Discounting all the time. It tells customers the full price is not real.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need my own website, or is a marketplace enough?

A marketplace brings visitors but sets the rules and puts competitors next to you. Your own website gives you control over the page, the story and the customer emails. Many small brands start on one and add the other, keeping the same name, look and voice on both.

How much should I put into branding before launch?

Enough for a clear position, a name and simple identity, good product photos and a card in every parcel. Custom boxes and a bigger website can grow with your sales.

Can I use AI to write product descriptions?

Yes, for first drafts. Then check every fact, add details only you know and edit the text into your own voice, so your store does not sound like everyone else's.

How do I get my first reviews?

Ask your first customers personally, a few days after delivery, with one direct link. Ask everyone, and never offer a reward for a positive review.
