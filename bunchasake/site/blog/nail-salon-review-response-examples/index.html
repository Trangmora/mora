Nail salon review response examples for every kind of review

A good reply is short, uses the client's name, mentions one real detail and, when something went wrong, says what you will do about it. Here are templates for each situation, ready to adapt.

By the Bunchasake team · Published 11 Nov 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • You reply to a review for the next client who reads it, not to win an argument with the person who wrote it.
  • Every reply has four parts: thank the client by name, mention one real detail, own any problem, and say what happens next.
  • For a complaint, apologise for the experience, move the details to a phone call and never discuss the visit in public.
  • Change a few words in every template so that no two replies on your page read the same.
  • One person, one fixed time each week and a wait-until-tomorrow rule for angry reviews keep replies calm and consistent.

The nail salon review response examples below cover the reviews a salon really gets: five stars with a long story, five stars with no words, a four-star with a small complaint, a one-star about waiting, and the review from someone you have never seen. Each template is two to four sentences, because that is all a reply needs. Copy them, change the words in brackets, and adjust the tone until it sounds like your salon.

If you still need to set up the listing itself, start with our guide to Google Business Profile for nail salons. This article is only about what to write once the reviews arrive.

Who you are really writing to

The person who wrote the review may never read your answer. The person choosing between you and the salon across the road will. They scroll to the lowest ratings on purpose, to see what goes wrong and how the owner behaves when it does. A calm, specific reply under a harsh review can do more for you than ten perfect ratings with no answer.

So write every reply for that silent reader. They want to know three things: do you listen, are you polite under pressure, and do you fix problems.

The four-part reply

  1. Thank them by name. Use the name shown on the review, nothing more.
  2. Mention one real detail. The service, the technician, the occasion. This shows a person wrote the reply.
  3. Own the problem, if there is one. Apologise for the experience without excuses.
  4. Say what happens next. For a happy client, an invitation to come back. For an unhappy one, a name and a phone number to sort it out in private.

Only mention what the client already wrote. Do not add details of their visit, their payment or their health, even to defend yourself.

Nail salon review response examples for happy clients

Five stars with a detailed review

Thank you, [Name]. We are so glad the builder gel is holding up and that [Technician] took the time to get the shape right. See you at your next fill.

Five stars with no text

Thank you for the five stars, [Name]. It means a lot to a small salon like ours. We look forward to seeing you again.

A review that praises one technician

Thank you, [Name]. We have passed your words on to [Technician], and they made her day. Ask for her by name any time you book.

Four stars with a small complaint

Thank you, [Name]. We are glad you love the colour, and you are right that the wait was longer than it should have been on Saturday. We have added a technician on weekend afternoons. We hope to earn the fifth star next time.

Only promise a change you have really made. If nothing has changed yet, write "we are looking at how we schedule weekends".

Examples for mixed and negative reviews

A long wait, even with an appointment

[Name], we are sorry you waited so long past your appointment time. That is not how we want a visit to begin. Please call us on [number] and ask for [Owner], so we can make your next booking right.

Nails that chipped or lifted within days

We are sorry to hear this, [Name]. A set should last longer than that. Please come by or call [number] within the week, and we will look at it and fix it.

If you have a written repair policy, put it on the price menu, so the offer in the reply matches what the front desk says.

A surprise at the register

Thank you for telling us, [Name], and we apologise for the confusion over the price. Nobody should be surprised when they pay. We are making sure extras are quoted before the service starts. Please call [number] if you would like to go through your bill with us.

The client felt rushed or unwelcome

[Name], we are sorry you felt rushed. Every client should feel looked after from the door to the dryer. We have shared your review with the team, and we would be glad of a chance to do better.

A comment about cleanliness

Thank you for raising this, [Name]. Clean tools and stations matter to us as much as they do to you, and we are sorry your visit left any doubt. We have reviewed this with the whole team. You are welcome to ask how we clean our tools at any visit.

Describe your cleaning routine only in words that are true every day.

A claim of a cut, a reaction or an infection

[Name], we are sorry to hear this and we take it seriously. Please call [number] and ask for [Owner], so we can understand what happened and talk with you directly.

Stop there. Do not guess at causes, argue or give health advice in public. Before you write more, speak to your insurer or a qualified adviser, because the rules differ by country and state.

Examples for unfair or awkward reviews

You cannot find the person in your records

Hello [Name]. We cannot find a visit under this name, and we want to understand what happened. Please call [number] with the date of your visit so we can look into it.

Then use the report option on the platform. The Google Business Profile Help Center explains how to flag a review, and Google decides whether it stays. Do not accuse the reviewer of lying in your reply.

The review is about another salon

Hello [Name]. We think this review may be meant for a different salon, as we do not offer that service. If we have this wrong, please call [number] and we will gladly help.

Anger about a deposit or a late cancellation fee

[Name], we understand the fee was frustrating. We hold a deposit because a missed appointment leaves a technician without work for that hour, and the policy is shown when booking. Please call [number], and we will see what we can do for your next visit.

A review in Vietnamese or another language

Reply in the language the client used, and keep it as short as the English versions. For a Vietnamese client:

Cảm ơn chị [Tên] đã ghé tiệm và để lại review. Tiệm rất vui vì chị thích bộ móng. Hẹn gặp lại chị lần sau.

A simple routine for replies

  • One person replies. The owner or the manager, so every answer sounds like the same salon.
  • One fixed time. For example Monday morning before the first client, for all platforms: Google, Facebook and, if you are listed there, Yelp for Business.
  • Wait until tomorrow for angry reviews. Draft it, sleep on it, and ask someone to read it before you post.
  • Fix first, then reply. Ask the technician what happened, so the reply can say something true.
  • Keep the templates in one note on the salon phone, and change a few words each time.

The tone of these replies is part of your brand voice, the same voice clients should hear at the front desk and read on your menu. Common mistakes

  • Arguing in public. You may be right, and you will still look like the salon that fights with clients.
  • Pasting the same reply everywhere. Twenty identical thank-yous look automatic.
  • Sharing details of the visit. What they had done, what they paid, what they said. Privacy rules may also apply where you are.
  • Offering a gift to change or remove a review. Platforms do not allow it, and consumer rules can apply too. In the US, the FTC business guidance covers reviews.
  • Answering only the bad ones. Happy clients deserve a thank-you too.
  • Replying weeks later. An old unanswered complaint looks like you do not care.

Frequently asked questions

Should I reply to every review, even ratings with no text?

Yes. A one-line thank-you with the client's name is enough. It shows future clients that someone is paying attention.

How quickly should I answer a negative review?

Within a few days, but not in the first hour. Find out what happened, write a draft, wait until the next day and post the calm version.

Can I ask a client to change their review after I fix the problem?

You can fix the problem and let them know you would welcome an update if they feel it is fair. Do not push, and never offer money, a discount or a free service in exchange.

What if a competitor or a stranger leaves a fake review?

Reply once, politely, saying you cannot find the visit and asking them to call. Then report the review through the platform and let the platform decide. And keep collecting real reviews.

My English is not strong. Is it fine to use templates?

Yes. Short, polite and correct beats long and creative. Ask someone you trust to check your templates once, then change the name and one detail each time you use them.
